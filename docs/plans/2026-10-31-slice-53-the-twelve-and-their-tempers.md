# Slice 53 — The twelve and their tempers

*31 Oct 2026. Phase 7, "Beloved of the gods" (`docs/specs/2026-10-31-beloved-of-the-gods-design.md`). The Olympus line's first slice (wave 1), built in the worktree `/home/claude/zeus-olympus` on `claude/p7-olympus`, beside the Temples, Legends and Epiphany lines. It starts at `11f9290` (the groundwork: the twelve's table and the mood API, state version 48).*

## Goal and check-in

The spec's words: dedicating shrines; what each god loves and hates read from the city; rivalry; neglect; festivals at home with a procession; offerings carried to the shrine; the Olympus panel (G) with the twelve's cards, moods and reasons; the overview's Gods page; the priestess in her god's colour.

The groundwork made the twelve and a mood API that nothing moved. This slice makes the moods move, from what the player does (dedicating shrines, festivals, offerings) and from what the city already is (granaries, theatres, the court, the army, trade, health, crime, the law, the herds, fires, tribute, quarantine), and shows them: on a panel of twelve cards, on the overview, and in the streets (a priestess in her god's colour, a procession, a porter carrying an offering).

**Oliver can:** open `#gods` and press G to read the twelve's moods and their reasons (Poseidon honoured, Athena jealous); click a shrine to dedicate it, then watch the reasons change; open `#festival` and follow Demeter's procession round the agora; hold a festival or send an offering from a shrine's panel.

**Demo links** (bare hash tokens):

- `#gods`: a mid-sized prepared town (`#town`'s, 28 months in) with shrines to four gods (Poseidon twice, Demeter, Athena and Apollo), an offering to Poseidon on its way, the Olympus panel open, and one rivalry biting: Poseidon honoured, Athena jealous. Moods staged with 'staged' memories where a year of play would put them.
- `#festival`: the same town with a festival to Demeter under way: her procession of white and gold walking from her shrine round the agora, the homes it has passed glad, and the shrine's panel open.
- `#gods-paused`, `#gods-plain`, `#gods-modern` and the same for `#festival`, as for every demo.
- Every earlier token keeps working, and opens exactly as before.

## Design decisions

**Where it lives.** `src/sim/worship.ts` (new): the numbers' table at its top, the honours from worship and their rivalry, the loves and hates read from the city, neglect, dedication, festivals and offerings, their walkers, and the monthly record of moods. `pantheon.ts` gains a block of fields on `Gods` (festivals, offerings, the last of each per god, a monthly record, the homes a procession passed) and the one reading `folk.ts` needs (a home's festival mood), so `folk.ts` imports only a leaf. `src/ui/olympus.ts` (new): the Olympus panel, the Gods page of the overview, the shrine's panel section; `src/ui/emblems.ts` (new): the twelve emblems drawn in SVG.

**Worship and rivalry.** Every honour from worship goes through one function, `worship(s, god, amount, kind, why, fade)`: it honours the god and, if the god's rival is honoured less (their mood is lower), gives the rival a memory of a third as much against you ("you honoured Poseidon, her rival"), with the same fade. A standing honour (shrines) goes through `standing(s, god, kind, why, amount)`, which asserts it and asserts the rival's standing grudge the same way, re-checked monthly. Only worship's kinds (shrine, festival, offering, and later sanctuary) call these; staged, neglect and the city's loves and hates never cause rivalry.

**Shrines dedicated.** A command `dedicate` (a shrine's id and a god, or none for "all the gods"). A shrine placed from now opens its panel with the twelve to choose from (and "all the gods"); older shrines stay to all the gods and honour none. A dedicated shrine still blesses homes as before. Each god's shrines are one standing honour, diminishing: 5 for the first, then 3, 2, and 1 for each after, at most 12. Only staffed shrines count. The priestess's robe takes her god's colour, walking and at her altar, in all three looks.

**Loves and hates read from the city.** Once a month per god, staggered (the twelve on days 1, 3, 5 … 23), each love and hate the city can show is re-asserted as a named reason with a modest weight (1 to 4), or forgotten when it no longer holds. Read only from what exists (examples): Demeter's full granaries, farms, bread on every table, hunger, idle fields; Zeus's court, a great city, a city of 1,000 without a court, little crime; Hera's full houses, loyal colonies and vassals; Poseidon's quay and sea routes, triremes, a coastal city with no quay; Athena's olive groves, theatres, a court; Apollo's theatres, physicians, a healthy city, plague without quarantine; Artemis' wild herds whole or hunted thin; Ares' army, tribute paid; Aphrodite's happy homes, estates, festivals, misery, executions in the law; Hephaestus' armouries, mines, quarries, monuments, fires burning; Hermes' routes, trade sold, a quarantine; Dionysus' vineyards and wineries, theatres, festivals, a sober city. Nothing is read before the city is noticed (300 people). The weights sit in one table at the top of `worship.ts`, tuned by playing `#town` and `#order` on: a city doing nothing special for a god sits about watchful, and a well-run city with a shrine to a god gets them to pleased within a year.

**Neglect.** Once the city has been noticed for a year, each month a god with no dedicated shrine, no festival and no offering in the last year slides: a memory "no shrine, festival or offering in a year" that grows by 2 a month and fades over a year once you honour them, never taking the mood below displeased (-29) by itself.

**Festivals at home.** A command `festival` (a shrine's id) from a dedicated shrine's panel: it costs coin (300 dr, through the treasury, its own ledger kind 'worship') and a measure of the god's sacred good taken from the stores at once (20; the panel says what is missing if not). One per god a year. It is announced a month ahead (a message) and on the day a procession of eight in the god's colour walks from the shrine to the nearest agora, round it and back, at a stately pace. Homes beside its way are glad for a season (+2, "the procession for Demeter passed their door"). On the day the god is honoured by 15, fading over a year, and the news says so. A *game* week is only 70 ticks (seven seconds at normal speed), too short to walk anywhere, so the procession lasts as long as its walk takes (about two to four weeks in a town), at most two months. `festivalHeld(s, god)` (the tick of the god's last festival, or -1) and `festivalToday(s, god)` are exported for slice 56's visit.

**Offerings.** A command `offer` (a shrine's id): a porter carries a measure (10) of the god's sacred good from the nearest store holding it, by road, to the shrine, and the honour (4, fading over six months) lands when he arrives. One at a time per shrine. Said plainly when no store holds the good or no road reaches it.

**The Olympus panel** (G, and a button on the top bar beside the overview's with a drawn bolt; the stores tool moves to Shift+G, as the water overlay moved to Shift+O). Twelve cards, four by three, readable at 1920 × 1080 without scrolling: the emblem drawn in the god's colour, the name and epithet, the mood word and a bar from furious to loving, the two weightiest reasons, the shrine count and the sanctuary slot ("no sanctuary yet"). A card opens the god's detail: every reason with its worth, what they love and hate, their rival and how the rivalry stands, their shrines (each a link), the festival and offering buttons (which act at the god's first staffed shrine), when the last festival was, and what they can do in blessing and in wrath (the table's words; slice 57 makes them real).

**The overview's Gods page**: the twelve's moods now, each with two years of monthly bars from the record and a link to its shrines.

**Messages:** a new kind 'gods' (filter City) for festivals announced and held, offerings given, and a god's mood crossing a word (pleased, warm, displeased, angry), each naming its reasons.

**What was built, as designed above, with these differences:**

- **All dedicated shrines count, not only staffed ones.** Dedicating a shrine changes its god's reasons at once, which is what Oliver will watch for; a shrine that loses its staff still stands to its god. (Easy to change: `shrinesTo` in `worship.ts`.)
- **A festival costs 250 dr, not 300**, so a town of about 900 like `#richtown` can hold one without waiting a year for the coin. Its sacred good: 20 of a measure (4 horses, 8 sets of armour); an offering 10 (2 horses, 4 sets).
- **The procession** is one walker of eight figures (as a settler household is), drawn in its own look: robed, mantled and veiled all in one colour, the god's and white by turns, each with a bowl and its flame. It walks from the shrine to the nearest agora within 40 road steps, round the agora's roadside, on to the road furthest from both within 14 steps of the agora, and home by the shortest way; with no agora, out to the furthest road within 14 steps and back. It walks at 0.8 of a walker's pace and lasts as long as its way takes (at most two months): a *game* week (70 ticks) is too short to walk anywhere. Each home beside its way is glad for a season (+2, "The procession for Demeter passed their door").
- **The hook for slice 56** is `festivalHeld(s, god)` (the tick the god's last festival began, or -1) and `festivalUnderWay(s, god)` (the procession is walking), not `festivalToday`.
- **The demos:** `#festival` was already Athens' Panathenaia (slice 21), so Demeter's festival is **`#procession`**. Both are built on `#richtown` (about 900 people, a quay and vineyards), not `#town`. `#gods` has shrines to Poseidon (two: the core's and the quay's), Demeter, Apollo and Dionysus; Athena has none, and is jealous of Poseidon. Poseidon's festival is held (its four horses brought into the quay's storehouse for the demo: the town keeps none), so the offering on its way is Demeter's (wheat from the granary), not Poseidon's. Athena's jealousy is staged a little deeper (-12, "set up for this demo"); everything else in both demos is played.
- **Mood crossings** (pleased, warm, displeased, angry and the rest) are news; a fall to displeased or worse is a warning (warnings don't pop up). In a city that never dedicates a shrine, neglect takes all twelve below watchful at about the same time, a year and a half after the city is noticed, so that month brings a dozen such warnings.
- **The Olympus panel's cards** also carry the god's blessing and wrath in a line each (the table's words; slice 57 makes them real), clipped to two lines; the god's page has them in full.
- **Shrines are named** by their district and their nearest landmark ("the shrine below the cypress hill, by the wheat farm"), so a city's shrines can be told apart in the panel, the overview and the messages.
- **G is Olympus.** The stores' tool (granary, storehouse, trading post, caravan yard) is Shift+G, cycling forward (Shift no longer cycles it backwards); the build bar's keys, the help, the overview's and the trade panel's hints say Shift G.
- **The overview's tabs** are a little tighter (3 px apart, 7 px of room on their right) so nine fit at 1920 wide.
- **The gods read the city, never the annals.** Aphrodite's happy homes and Zeus's "little crime" are counted from the city as it stands (homes glad this month, last month's crimes by the court's rate), not from `annals.ts`: the annals are only a record, and `tests/annals.test.ts` checks that keeping them never changes how a town plays. (First built reading the annals; that test caught it.)
- **Saves:** the per-god records (`worshipped`, `lastFestival`, `months`) start empty and fill as they are needed, so a save of a town whose gods have never watched grows by 85 characters; `#town`'s save budget in `tests/saves.test.ts` goes from 194,500 to 194,600.
