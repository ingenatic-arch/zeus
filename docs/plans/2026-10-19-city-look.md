# Plan: the city's new look (side slice, unnumbered)

Oliver's ask (19 Oct): the city's textures look like a tacky browser game; make them match the new lit-marble UI. He picked look **A, Sunlit terracotta** from the mock-ups, then asked for better ground, trees and people (second round). Mock-ups: `/mnt/project-files/zeus-city-look/` (round one) and `round2/`. Stage 1 lives in `src/render/finish.ts`; the stage-2 trees and people are still lab code (`lookTrees.ts`, `lookFolk.ts`, on only when `window.__look = 'lab'`).

This is render-only: no sim state changes, no save version bump, determinism untouched.

## What gets built

1. **Light and grade.** A lower sky fill and a stronger, warmer sun; softer shadows; ambient occlusion so buildings sit on the ground; a light bloom; a warm grade with a soft vignette. Post-processing goes through one composer in `main.ts`.
2. **Ground.** Smooth ground (corners share their normals and colours in `terrain.ts`) instead of triangle facets. Grass in tufts with dry gold drifts and bare soil, wind ripples on the sand, bedding on rock, all drawn in the shader and faded to an average far away so it never shimmers. Roads get flagstones; plots get trodden earth.
3. **Building materials.** Tiled roofs with rows and channels that catch the light, plaster walls with grain and dust at the foot, slabs on paving and steps. Roof tiles are picked out by their exact palette colours (passed to the shader), so awnings and pots are never tiled by mistake; walls, slabs and leaves are told apart by colour.
4. **Trees and shrubs.** New olive, stone pine, cypress, scrub and broom: crowns built from many leaf clumps that shade as one soft mass, with boughs reaching into them. The trees inside building models (courtyard cypresses, gardens) use the same models.
5. **People.** A figure kit (head with nose and hair, neck, waist, belted tunic or long dress in folds, two-part arms with hands, shins and sandals) used by every walker, not just the four the mock-up covered. The merged walker mesh is split by size so a big figure doesn't make every small one pay for its vertices.
6. **Water and colour.** A deeper Aegean sea with turquoise only in the shallows, softer greens, a softer foam edge on the shore instead of the hard white band, and hearth smoke that drifts instead of white bubbles.
7. **Building details (not in the mock-ups yet).** Cornices, door frames, window reveals and a base course on homes and the grand buildings, so the walls stop reading as plain boxes. Oliver sees this in a screenshot before it is built into every model.

## How it is staged

- **Stage 1:** light and grade, ground, building materials, water and colour (items 1, 2, 3, 6).
- **Stage 2:** trees and people (items 4, 5).
- **Stage 3:** building details (item 7), after Oliver has seen a mock-up of them.

Each stage leaves the game playable and is published to the usual artifact link. Demo: `#goods` shows the town in the new look; adding `-plain` to any link (for example `#goods-plain`) shows the old look for comparison until the stage is signed off.

## Budgets and checks

- Measure the frame time on the #goods town with `npm run measure` on real graphics before each stage merges. The ambient occlusion and the extra triangles in trees and people are the expensive parts. If they miss the 60 fps target, the fallback is a Graphics setting (high or low) that turns off ambient occlusion and bloom and uses simpler trees far away.
- No new tests: this is visual and judged by looking. The existing determinism, save and routing tests must still pass.
- Screenshots of every demo view before and after, tuned by eye, then Oliver judges.

## Working alongside the other threads

The side panel thread owns the UI files and phase 4 owns the sim on main. This work touches only `src/render/`, `main.ts`'s renderer setup and `palette.ts`. It is built on its own branch and merged to main the way the side panel was.
