## Goal and check-in

The spec's words: Oliver's choice, "Conquer, cow or buy; plus colonies". Three ways to take a city: **conquest** (a full force sent "to conquer" meets the city's whole army; won, the city is your vassal), **awe** (a cold or wary city with a much smaller army can be told to submit: it bows, refuses and remembers it, or calls on its friends; the odds shown with their reasons before you send), and **debt** (a city that owes you more than it can repay can be offered its debt for its loyalty). Vassals run lightly: a page with its tribute (coin or one of its goods, light, fair or heavy) and its loyalty with every reason; tribute arriving as caravans or ships into your stores and treasury; a vassal's company coming down its road when you are raided; a vassal never trading against you. A rebellion is telegraphed a season ahead, and you can lighten the tribute, send a gift or march. Allies: a warm ruler can be offered an alliance; aid both ways, and each asks the other to join its wars, remembered if refused. Balance card: a lender's army comes back only for a remainder worth the march.

At the end of slice 31 Greece's rulers take vassals of their own while your city can only trade, lend, give and fight. After this slice your city can stand over its neighbours: Argos bowing to your army, Corinth's unpaid debt turned into a yearly train of donkeys walking oil into your storehouse, Sparta's men coming down the west road when Troy's raiders land. And it can lose them again, with a season's warning.

**Oliver can:** open `#vassal` and watch Corinth's tribute caravan come down the north road into the storehouse, the jars in the stock book and the coin in the ledger; set its tribute heavier or lighter and see its loyalty move with the reasons; open `#submit` and read the odds of making Argos bow before he sends the herald; open `#conquer` and read the report of the conquest; open `#rebellion` and act on a vassal arming against him.

## Design decisions

**State** (`empire`, one top-level field; types in `state.ts`' Slice 34 block, logic in `src/sim/vassals.ts`). The vassal link itself is slice 31's: a vassal's `lord` is `'home'`, `lordSince` when it bowed. `empire` keeps what is yours alone: each vassal's record (how it was taken, its tribute, its loyalty, a rebellion's warning and day, when its next tribute leaves), each ally's (since when, refusals each way), heralds on their way with a demand or an offer (and their answers, kept a year), tribute on its way, aid on its way, and an ally's call to join its war. Expeditions gain an `aim` ('' as before, 'conquer', or 'war' when joining an ally's war); an allied company in your city carries `ally` (the city it came from).

**Three ways to take a city.**
- **Conquest.** A "Send a full force to conquer" button on a ruled city's Empire tab (its odds and reasons shown, as an expedition's are). The force musters, marches and fights as slice 28's does, but against the city's whole army on its home ground: its army (`garrisonOf`, which is the army as it stands) a quarter again for fighting at home, walls as before (Troy half again), and help: each friend (a ruler at +30 or better with it, and with a way between them) sends a quarter of its army, and a rival lord defends its vassal with a quarter of its own. Your allies with a road may march with you (their dice; warm 6 in 10, devoted 8 in 10), adding a quarter of their army to your side. Won: the city bows to you (vassal, "by conquest"), pays nothing that day (the plunder of a slice 28 force without a debt still comes home), loses people and wealth as a defeat in war, and remembers it hard (−40, fading over four years); Greece is shocked as at a force without cause. Lost: as a lost force, and it remembers the attempt.
- **Awe.** A city cold or wary to you (hostile is too angry to listen; cordial or warmer is offered an alliance instead), its side at most half your army (your men ready, against its army, a quarter of each friend's and of its lord's), can be sent a herald: "submit to us". The odds: 15 in 100 at twice its side, 20 in 100 more for each time over that, less for a proud (−15), warlike (−15) or steadfast (−10) ruler and for a cold one (−10), more for a shrewd one (+10) and one you beat in the last two years (+15), −10 for each friend who would stand by it; between 5 and 90 in 100. Shown before sending as a word (likely, fair, doubtful, unlikely) with the reasons ("your 72 men against its 20 and Sparta's 15; Sparta might come to its aid; proud"). The herald goes and comes back (a day per two of the road, 10 to 40 days); the answer: it **bows** (vassal "by awe"); it **refuses** and remembers being threatened (−20, two years); or, if it has a friend, it **calls on its friends**: the friend takes it ill (−15 with you), and a cold, proud or warlike friend may threaten you (raids.ts' threat, if Greece has none live). One demand a city a year.
- **Debt.** A city whose loan to you is overdue and which owes more than it can repay (its treasury under the debt, or doubtful to pay) can be offered "your debt for your loyalty" on the Loans tab beside forgive and demand. Odds from how much it owes against its treasury, its mood (cordial or better +10, cold −20), proud −20, greedy +10, and its fear of your army (loans.ts' `fearOf`); shown before sending. Accepted: the loan ends as forgiven ("for its loyalty"), the city is your vassal "by debt" and starts kindly (+10 loyalty, fading over three years). Refused: the debt stands, a small grudge (−5).
- **A rival's vassal** (Argos under Sparta, say) can be taken by conquest or awe: its lord's quarter army stands by it, and its lord remembers the taking (−20 with you). Taking it over is the one way to free it from a rival; there is no "free it" act (simplest, and a freed city with nothing owed to you is no gain).

**Vassals, run lightly.**
- **Tribute**, set on the vassal's page: coin, or one of the goods it sells you; **light** (a tenth of its yearly income), **fair** (a fifth, the default) or **heavy** (three tenths). Its income is slice 31's (its base wealth × 3% a month, with its people). Sent each season (a quarter of the year's), the first a month after it bows. Coin comes out of its treasury, goods out of its stock at its price (a measure is what its price says a hundredth of the coin buys); a vassal short of coin pays what it can, and says so.
- **The caravan.** By road (Corinth, Sparta, Argos, Thebes; Athens by road too): a caravan in the vassal's colours on the map of Greece along its road, then donkeys into your city by its road edge, along the roads to the nearest store with room (a storehouse or granary for goods; the tax office for coin, or the nearest store if there is none), where they unload: the goods into the store (spilling into the next of its kind, what nothing has room for lost and named), the coin into the treasury. Then they walk out again. By sea only (Troy, Knossos, Rhodes, Memphis): a ship in its colours sails in and anchors short of the jetty, and its cargo comes ashore at the trading post and walks to the store as a caravan; with no trading post, it is unloaded on the beach straight into the stores, named so. With no road from the edge to any store: the goods are left at the edge and put straight into the stores, named so. The ledger has a line kind of its own ("Tribute from vassals"), the stock book a line per vassal ("tribute from Corinth"). The hover on the caravan says whose tribute it is and what it carries.
- **Loyalty** (0 to 100), moved each month half the way towards a target that is the sum of named reasons, each shown on the page with its weight: a base of 50; the tribute (light +15, fair 0, heavy −20); how it was taken (by conquest −15 fading over three years, by awe −5 over two, by debt +10 over three); time under you (+3 a year, at most +15); your army against its own (your men ready or away against its army: from −15 when it matches you to +20 at four times); your army abroad (−10 while an expedition is out); your gifts (what its ruler remembers of them, three fifths of it, at most +20); its ruler's mood otherwise (a fifth, ±10); and whispers (−5 for each rival cold to you and friendly with it, at most −15). The page lists them, biggest first: "Loyalty 34: heavy tribute −20; your army abroad −10; by conquest −12; ...".
- **Rebellion, a season ahead.** Loyalty under 30 at a month's turn: the vassal arms. Urgent news and a line under the top bar: "Corinth is arming: loyalty 24 (heavy tribute, your army abroad). It rises within a season unless its loyalty mends." It names the day, 90 days on. Three answers on its page: **lighten the tribute** (the target rises at once, the loyalty at the next months' turns), **send a gift** (the Ruler tab's gift, which counts at once in the gifts' reason), or **march** (a full force "to crush it": if it wins, the rising is crushed, loyalty set back to 50 and a hard memory; if it loses, the city rises at once). Loyalty back to 40 or over at a month's turn: it stands down (news). On the day, under 30: it **rises**: no longer your vassal, a grudge (−25, three years: "threw off your rule"), and it is a rival again.
- **Aid when you are raided.** When a band gathers against your city, each vassal and ally with a road to you (not the raider itself, not at war) sends a company: about a quarter of its army, 8 to 16 men, marching down its road on the map of Greece and walking in from its road edge to stand by where the band is going (raids.ts' `landing`); it fights the raiders as a company holding there does, and walks home when the band has gone or if it breaks. A vassal or ally with no road to you sends no men (the sea is slice 38's), and its page says so. An allied company costs you nothing and is not yours to send abroad.
- **A vassal never trades against you:** its route stays open whatever its mood (unless it seizes your goods over a debt of yours), its traders are never slowed, and its terms are never worse than a cordial ruler's. Your vassals and allies never threaten you or demand tribute.

**Allies.** A warm (+30) or devoted ruler can be offered an alliance (a herald, as above): warm 55 in 100, devoted 85, steadfast +10, fickle −15, proud −10. An alliance ends if its ruler's mood falls to cold, after two refusals of its calls within three years, or if you end it (−20 with that ruler). **Its calls:** when an ally goes to war with another rival, it asks you to join (once a war, 60 days to answer): **join** (a full force sent against its foe, as an expedition with aim "war": if it wins, the war ends in your ally's favour on the war's day) or **refuse** (−15 with your ally, remembered on the alliance). **Your calls:** a conquest asks each ally with a road to march with you; one that declines is remembered on the alliance's record ("Thebes would not march with you against Argos").

**The lender's army** (balance card). A lender's army no longer comes for a remainder under 500 dr (`DEBT_WORTH` in loans.ts): no army at step 3, and none again after one has left, while what is left is under it. The debt's line says so: "not worth a march: 120 dr left; they keep seizing your goods on their routes until it is paid".

**Rival vassals** (finding of slice 31). With base armies, Sparta's is more than two and a half times Argos' and Memphis' more than Rhodes', so the same two bows came in most runs. Tuned by a probe (outside the repo) over many seeds and twelve years of Greece alone, before and after, so that bows are rarer and more varied: an army three times the other's, the two at a feud (−25 or worse) or a war won within two years, one month in 48. The numbers go in *As built*.

**The page** (`src/ui/greece.ts` and a new section of the line's UI). A ruled city gets an **Empire** tab: as a vassal, its tribute (coin or a good, light, fair or heavy, each with what it brings a year), what it has sent and what is on its way, its loyalty with every reason, a rebellion's warning and the three answers, and "when you are raided" (what it would send); as an ally, since when, its calls and yours, "End the alliance"; otherwise the ways to take it, each with its odds and reasons or why not (submit, conquer, offer an alliance), and the last herald's answer or conquest report. The map's labels carry a small mark for your vassals and allies, and Greece's news says what happened. The Rivals tab's "How it stands" names your city as its lord.

## Tests

- **Determinism** (existing, with the new demos among the prepared towns): the same seed and commands give the same city and Greece.
- **Each way to take a city:** a conquest won makes a vassal (and a lost one does not); a submit demand's answer follows its odds over many dice (bows, refuses, calls on friends), and is refused for a hostile or a cordial ruler, or with too small an army; a debt for loyalty accepted ends the loan as forgiven and makes a vassal.
- **Loyalty:** heavier tribute lowers it, lighter raises it, a gift raises it; each reason is named; time and your army count.
- **Rebellion is warned first:** a vassal never rises without its warning a season before; it stands down when loyalty mends, rises on the day if not.
- **Tribute:** a caravan arrives by road into a store; the stock book and the ledger (the treasury invariant, `ledger.test.ts`'s) account for it; coin into the treasury; a sea-only vassal's tribute arrives too.
- **Aid:** a band gathering brings an ally's company into your city by its road, which leaves after the raid.
- **Allies' calls:** an ally at war asks; refusing is remembered; two refusals end it.
- **The lender's threshold:** a remainder under 500 dr brings no army, over it does.
- **Rival vassals:** over many seeds, bows are rarer than before and not all the same two (the probe's figures pinned loosely).
- **Save and load:** a town with vassals, an ally, tribute on its way and a warning round-trips; a version 27 save (the slice 31 fixture) loads through `fromSlice30`, `fromSlice31` and `fromSlice34` and plays on.
- The existing suite. `smoke.mjs` opens the four new demos; `measure.mjs` adds them.

## As built

Built as designed, with these differences (each my choice; Oliver can change any).

**The empire** lives in `src/sim/vassals.ts` (records, odds, tribute, loyalty, rebellion, aid, calls, the `empire` command) and its page in a new `src/ui/empire.ts` (the Empire tab). State version 30, `fromSlice34` filling `empire` and each expedition's `aim`.

**Differences from the design.**
- **The demos.** `#vassal` follows Corinth's tribute caravan in (the camera on its donkeys, the town line saying what it brings) rather than opening on the Empire tab; in `#collect`'s town Corinth's road comes in by the **west** edge, not the north. Corinth's ruler accepted the debt for his loyalty by the dice (7 in 10), so nothing is staged there. Its tribute is set **light, in wine** (it could not spare a fair season's worth of wine: 260 jars light against about 520 fair), so the donkeys carry jars. `#submit` is `#companies`' town with a second barracks and a band of Arcadians hired there (48 men), not `#fortress`'s (its Sparta band and walls would crowd the demo); `#conquer` is `#companies`' town (32 men against Argos' 26 at home: about 6 in 10, and it won). `#rebellion` is `#companies`' town with Corinth bowed by awe (**set up for this demo**, and named so in its news) and its tribute heavy; the army is at home (heavy tribute and an army too small to fear take loyalty under 30 at the first month's turn).
- **Awe odds** tuned so cowing Argos takes a real army: 25 in 100 at twice its side, 22 more for each time over (the design had 15 and 20). At `#submit` (48 men against Argos' 21, a proud king) it is about 2 in 10.
- **Conquest's grudge** is slice 28's for a full force without cause (the city −60 or worse, every other ruler −12 or more; `#conquer` shows −90 and −18), not a separate −40. A conquered city also loses as from a defeat (people −6%, army −40%, wealth −20%, in its size reasons as "conquered by your city"), and is **cowed** for a while: +25 loyalty fading over 18 months (+15 over a year for awe). Without it a conquered city armed against you the month after.
- **Herald answers** come back in twice his way, 10 to 60 days (the design said 10 to 40; Memphis is far).
- **Aid** is three tenths of the vassal's or ally's army, 8 to 16 men. A company that breaks goes home and its line says so.
- **Tribute caravans** walk at a herald's pace (`SENDING_SPEED`), not a trader's: at a trader's pace #collect's 92-segment road took about 80 days.
- **Debt for loyalty** is offered for any overdue loan with nobody out after it (not only one larger than the treasury); how much it owes against its treasury moves the odds instead. It is on the Empire tab and beside Forgive and Demand on the Loans tab.
- **Allies' help both ways**: they send men when you are raided; you answer their calls to war; they may march with your conquests (their dice). There is no herald figure on the map (as planned).
- **Rival vassals.** The design's rule (an army three times the other's, a feud, one month in 48) still had Argos bow to Sparta in most runs. As built: unless the vassal lost a war lately, a rival takes it only when its army is under four fifths of its usual and the two are at a feud (−25 or worse); and a lord a city broke free of cannot take it again for four years. The odds and the strength ratio are slice 31's (2.5 times, one month in 30). Probe (Greece alone, 40 seeds, 12 years, `new Sim(seed)`): before 83 bows, Argos to Sparta 54 (in 34 runs of 40), Rhodes to Memphis 7; after **41 bows, Argos to Sparta 25 (in 22 runs)**, Athens to Sparta 5, Corinth to Troy 3, seven other pairs 8. Argos is still the likeliest (small, beside Sparta, often at war with it): Oliver's eye.
- **Fixed on the way:** slice 28's `edgeFor` tried only the ten nearest edge tiles on the city's side, all unreachable in `#collect` for the west edge; it now samples the rest too (a force sent to Argos from `#collect` went nowhere before).
- **Drawn in the city:** an ally's or vassal's company wears its city's colour and carries its city's banner (its letter and name); tribute donkeys carrying oil or wine have jars in rope cradles; a sea vassal's tribute ship sails into the bay and rides at anchor. On the map of Greece: tribute caravans and ships, aid companies, a ring in your colour on an ally's label, a shield on a vassal arming, a horn on an ally's call; the Empire tab opens from any of them.

**Earlier demos.** Compared at opening against the baseline build (the prepared towns' labels): every demo opens as before except `#rivals` (and `#rivalsmap`, `#rivalstreet`, `#lend`, `#repaid`, `#forgive`, built on it), `#fortress`, and `#greecegrows` (and `#knossos`, `#memphis`). Each moved because Greece plays differently with the rival-vassal rule (checked by probe: with slice 31's rule put back, `#rivals` and `#fortress` open exactly as the baseline). `#rivals` waits for Greece's war to end before staging Troy against Athens: a month later (July 746 BC, was June; 1,411 people as before; 4,846 dr, was 3,410); its descendants move with it. `#fortress` waits on rulers' loans and a calm Greece: ready nine months sooner (June 745 BC, was March 744; 1,330 people, was 1,344; 3,057 dr, was 2,836). `#greecegrows`: no rival took a vassal in its ten years, so Argos' bow to Sparta is set up as its setup already does in that case (a day later; 1,313 people, was 1,301).
