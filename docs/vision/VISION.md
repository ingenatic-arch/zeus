# Vision: the Zeus remake (working title)

> A warm, living Greek city that you lay out street by street, pressed on every side by a mythic world of rulers, merchants, fleets and gods who remember what you did.

You found a city on a real stretch of the Greek coast. You lay out roads and houses, and walkers carry water, food and culture down the streets you drew; houses grow as their families' needs are met, and you can click any home to see who lives there and how they feel. Beyond your walls, Greece is alive. Athens, Sparta, Corinth and Troy are ruled by people with personalities and grudges, prices rise and fall with harvests and wars, triremes guard or raid the sea lanes, and the gods watch, pleased or jealous, ready to bless your harvest or send a monster to your gates. Building is calm and satisfying; the world brings pressure you can see coming; setbacks hurt but you recover. You pick an ambition (master of Greece, the great trading city, beloved of the gods) and play as long as you like.

## Pillars, in priority order

The order decides conflicts: when two pillars pull against each other, the higher one wins.

1. **The city feels alive.** The core of the game, and what everything else presses on.
   - Real time with pause. You watch the city tick, and pause to plan.
   - Zeus-style walkers carry services along the roads you lay out, so road layout is the puzzle.
   - Each house is a household with needs, a mood and a small story you can click into; houses grow (and shrink) with what reaches them.
   - Production chains feed storehouses and markets, as in the original.
2. **Warm, storybook Greece.** It should be a pleasure to look at and listen to.
   - Stylised low-poly 3D, warm and toy-like, with a camera you can rotate and zoom. All art is built in code, so every slice can look finished.
   - Bright Mediterranean colour; gentle humour in the voices of citizens, advisors, rulers and gods; a touch of the epic when a god arrives.
3. **A world that remembers.** Greece goes on around you, and its people hold grudges.
   - The real map of mythic Greece, with Egypt and Troy at the edges; rulers, personalities and starting conditions are shuffled each game.
   - Each ruler has a personality and goals, and remembers gifts, broken promises and refused requests. They act on it with trade deals, alliances, demands for tribute and war.
   - Readable on the surface: you can always see where you stand with someone and why.
   - You can found colonies or bring rivals under your rule, and run them lightly: set what they send you and watch their loyalty.
4. **A living market and the sea lanes.**
   - World prices respond to supply, war and harvests, so what you make and sell shifts over time.
   - Trade travels by land and sea, and sea lanes can be protected, blockaded or raided.
5. **War on land and sea.**
   - Invaders march onto your city map, and you command formations (hoplites, archers, chariots) through your own streets and walls in real time. How you built decides how you defend.
   - Expeditions abroad play out on the world map with a clear report.
   - Fleets are a core part of military strength: triremes guard trade routes, blockade and raid rivals, and carry armies. Sea battles resolve on the world map. At home, enemy fleets can blockade your harbour or land troops on your shore, and you fight off the landing yourself.
6. **Gods with feelings.** The gods are another kind of diplomacy.
   - They react to worship, festivals and your deeds in the wider world; honouring one can anger another.
   - They walk your streets, step into wars, send monsters and call for heroes.

**Ambitions** tie the pillars together: you choose a path, monuments and milestones mark your progress, and you can keep playing once you reach it.

## Feel

Calm building, rising stakes. Laying out the city is relaxing; trouble from the world is visible before it lands; setbacks hurt but are recoverable. Difficulty settings can stretch this either way later.

## Who it's for

Oliver first: a single-player game made to his taste. Getting it ready for other players is a later phase, if it turns out great.

## Out of scope (for now)

- Multiplayer.
- Authored campaigns and scenarios (the systems should allow them later).
- Modding.
- A desktop build: browser first, packaged for desktop once the core is solid.
- Real-time sea battles you command ship by ship (a possible later addition).

## What comes first

**A living neighbourhood.** Roads and houses on a 3D Greek coast, walkers carrying water and food, and houses that grow as their needs are met. It nails the core loop and the look before anything else presses on it.

## Touchstones

- **Zeus: Master of Olympus and Poseidon**: the heart of it, from walkers and housing to gods, heroes and monsters.
- **Pharaoh: A New Era**: a modern, handsome take on the same kind of city.
- **Townscaper**: the warm, toy-like low-poly look.
- **Crusader Kings**: rulers who remember and act on personal grudges.
- **Anno**: trade chains and sea lanes that matter.
