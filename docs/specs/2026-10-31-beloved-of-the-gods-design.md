# Phase 7: Beloved of the gods

*31 Oct 2026. Chosen by Oliver after phase 6 ("Crime, health and a polished city") on the next-phase card, then shaped in six questions (30 and 31 Oct). Nothing from playing phase 6 stood out to him, so the phase is all gods and the phase 6 leftovers stay recorded for later. It serves pillar 6, "Gods with feelings" ("The gods are another kind of diplomacy. They react to worship, festivals and your deeds in the wider world; honouring one can anger another. They walk your streets, step into wars, send monsters and call for heroes"), the vision's fourth ambition, *Beloved of the gods*, and the feel it asks for: "a touch of the epic when a god arrives" and "pressure you can see coming". Pillar 1, "The city feels alive", still wins every conflict: every god, monster and hero must show up as something that changes in the city.*

## Summary

At the end of phase 6 the city is deep and readable, but heaven is empty. Homes need a priestess's blessing from a nameless shrine ("Nobody's quite sure which god the shrine is for"), Greece's festivals are only prices, and the vision's biggest pillar has nothing in it.

This phase fills Olympus:
- **Twelve gods with feelings.** Zeus, Hera, Poseidon, Demeter, Athena, Apollo, Artemis, Ares, Aphrodite, Hephaestus, Hermes and Dionysus each watch your city, with a mood that names its reasons, things they love and hate, and a rival who minds when you honour the other.
- **Worship you build.** Shrines are dedicated to a god. Great sanctuaries rise in stages over a year or two, in marble and timber, as many as you dare. Festivals and offerings speak to a god directly.
- **Gods who answer.** A pleased god blesses the city; an angry one curses it, with real damage, always warned a month ahead by omens that name the god and the reason, and an offering can still turn it. The gods walk your streets, towering and glowing.
- **Monsters and heroes.** An angry god can send a monster to a lair on your land. Your army can wound it and drive it back, at a cost; only a hero can slay it, and a hero comes only when you raise their hall and meet their demands.
- **Gods in war, on both sides.** Greece's cities have patron gods. A god who loves you may answer your call and fight invaders in your streets, and a rival's patron can march with their army against you.
- **Beloved of the gods**, the fourth ambition, with its five milestones.

It is planned to be **built on four lines at once**, as phase 6 was (see *The parallel plan*).

- **A mood from -100 to 100**, made of memories that fade (as a ruler's mood is), shown as one of seven words (furious, angry, displeased, watchful, pleased, warm, loving) with every reason listed: "+18 your sanctuary to Demeter; +6 full granaries; -9 you honoured Zeus, her rival". Gods start watchful; a new city is beneath their notice until it has 300 people, so an early town is never cursed.
- **What moves a mood:** worship (shrines dedicated to them, their sanctuary, festivals, offerings of their sacred good), what they love and hate in the city (read from the systems that already exist: granaries, theatres, the court, the army, trade, health, crime, punishments), deeds in Greece (wars, tribute, broken promises, gifts, plunder of their patron city: slice 56), and neglect (a slow slide for a god never honoured, once the city is noticed).
- **Rivals:** honouring a god gives their rival a memory of a third as much against you, only while the rival is honoured less.
- **Shrines are dedicated.** A shrine has a god, chosen on its panel (a new shrine asks; an old one starts "to all the gods", which honours none of them and serves homes as before). A dedicated shrine still blesses homes as before, its priestess wears the god's colour, and each one honours its god a little.
- **Festivals at home.** From a shrine or sanctuary's panel you can hold a festival for its god: it costs coin and some of the god's sacred good, is announced a month ahead, and for a week a procession walks from the shrine round the agora and homes' moods lift. It honours the god a good deal and lifts every home nearby. One festival per god a year.
- **Offerings:** send a measure of the sacred good to a shrine or sanctuary (carried by its priestess's porters from a store): a small, quick honour, the way to turn a curse at the last moment.
- **The Olympus panel** (G, and a button on the top bar): the twelve as cards with their emblem drawn in their colour, their mood word and bar, the reasons, what they love and hate, their rival, their sanctuary's stage, and what they will do for you (blessings, slice 57) or are about to do to you (omens). Moods are kept month by month for the overview, which gets a ninth page, Gods.

**Sanctuaries rise (slice 54)**
- **One sanctuary per god per city, as many gods as you like.** Each is a 5×5 precinct: a stepped temple with columns, an altar before it, a walled court, trees and the god's statue, each god's in their own pose, colour and emblem (Poseidon's trident and horses, Demeter's sheaf and kore, Athena's owl and olive tree, Ares' spear and trophy, Dionysus's vines). They share one builder in code, with the god's parts on top.
- **Built in stages, as the monuments are:** marked out (a cost in coin), then foundations, the temple (columns), and the statue and finish. Each stage needs marble and timber on site (the statue also copper for bronze, and a measure of the god's sacred good to consecrate it), hauled by the ox carts and raised by the masons' builders, as monuments are. A fully supplied sanctuary takes about a year; one competing with others, about two.
- **While it rises** the god notices: each stage adds a memory. Finished, it is a large honour that never fades while it stands and is staffed, its priests walk out as the shrine's priestess does but further (serving homes' religion across a wide area), and it unlocks the god's blessings to call on (slice 57) and the god's home in the city (slice 58).
- **Its panel** shows the stages, the god's mood, the festival button and offerings, and later the blessing and the god's presence.
- **Neglect:** a sanctuary that loses its workers for a season dims and the honour fades until they return.

**Monsters at the gates (slice 55)**
- **Seven monsters, each from one god:** the Hydra (Hera), Medusa (Athena), the Cyclops (Poseidon), the Minotaur (Zeus), the Chimera (Ares), Talos the bronze giant (Hephaestus) and the Calydonian Boar (Artemis). The other gods curse without monsters (slice 57).
- **A monster takes a lair** at a wild spot on your land (a cave on a hillside, a marsh, a rocky shore for the Cyclops), drawn there, and is announced first: shepherds see it, then it settles in. Every few weeks it comes out and raids: it walks to a target its kind prefers (the Boar to fields and herds, the Cyclops to the quay and ships, Medusa to busy streets, where she turns walkers to stone, Talos to the walls and armouries, the Hydra to homes by water), wrecks or burns what it reaches and kills those who don't run, then goes home. Walkers near it flee indoors.
- **Your army can fight it**, man by man as battles are (battle.ts), but it has great health and hits very hard: a company can wound it and, when it loses enough, it breaks off and goes back to its lair to heal for a season. Soldiers who fight it fall. It never dies to soldiers.
- **Monster-sized figures**, drawn in code: several times a man's height, each with a silhouette you can tell at a glance.
- **A monster stays** until a hero slays it (slice 59), or its god's mood rises to warm and the god calls it home.

**The gods walk your streets (slice 56)**
- **A god's figure** is about three storeys tall, built in code from their parts (robes, armour, attributes), glowing in their colour, with a soft halo on the ground and a fading trail of light where they step. They walk over houses and roads, never blocked, at a stately pace.
- **Their arrival** is a flourish: Zeus in a lightning strike and thunder, Poseidon rising in sea spray at the shore, Demeter in a rain of petals over the fields, Ares in a crash of bronze, Hermes in a flash of wings, Dionysus with a ring of vines. The page's sound plays a short sting; a message says who has come and why; the camera doesn't move unless you click it.
- **Why they come** in this slice: to visit, when their mood first reaches loving, on their festival, or when their sanctuary is finished. A visiting god walks to the places they love (Demeter to the farms, Hephaestus to the armoury, Dionysus to the theatre), and each place they touch is blessed for a season in a small, visible way (a farm's field turns gold and yields more, the armoury's smiths work faster, the theatre fills).
- They stay a few days, then leave as they came. Slice 57 adds wrath visits; slice 60 adds war.
- **Readable:** hovering a god names them and says what they're doing ("Demeter walks your fields: pleased with your granaries").

**Blessings and wrath (slice 57)**
- **Blessings.** A god who is warm or better and has a finished sanctuary can be asked for their blessing once a year from the sanctuary's panel, and a loving god sometimes gives one unasked. Each god's is their own:
  - Zeus: a great gift of coin from the sky's treasury, and his lightning strikes any raiders on your map;
  - Hera: every colony's and vassal's loyalty rises, and homes have more children;
  - Poseidon: fair seas for a year (ships quicker, no storms, enemy ships slowed) and an earth tremor that clears rubble;
  - Demeter: every farm yields double for a season;
  - Athena: olive groves double for a season, and crime falls as people grow wise;
  - Apollo: every sick home is healed, and a plague ends at once;
  - Artemis: the wild herds swell, and hunters bring home double;
  - Ares: every company is filled and armoured for free;
  - Aphrodite: every home's mood rises for a season, and settlers come twice as often;
  - Hephaestus: the armouries and the masons work double for a season, and fires go out;
  - Hermes: your prices are better for a season, and caravans and porters walk quicker;
  - Dionysus: the wineries yield double, and a free festival for every home.
  The god walks the city to give it (slice 56's figure). The blessing's effect is shown where it lands (gold on the fields, a line on the panel and in the messages).
- **Wrath, always warned.** A god who is angry or furious, with a city noticed, chooses a curse. A month before it lands, omens begin: a message names the god and the reasons ("Ominous signs: owls flee the city and the olives drop unripe. Athena is angry: you honoured Poseidon, and the theatre has been empty for a year"), the god's card shows the curse and the day, and signs appear in the city (a dark cloud over the target, dead birds, a red sky at the shore). A sacrifice or offering of the sacred good, or a festival, that lifts the god back past angry turns the curse ("Athena is appeased").
- **Curses do real damage** (Oliver's choice):
  - Zeus: lightning strikes a few buildings, which catch fire;
  - Hera: the Hydra, and families quarrel and some leave the city;
  - Poseidon: an earthquake (walls, roads and buildings near the shore crack and fall), a ship lost at sea with her cargo, or the Cyclops;
  - Demeter: blight on the fields for a season, and a granary's grain rots;
  - Athena: Medusa, or the olives fail for a year;
  - Apollo: plague from his arrows, in the quarter he names;
  - Artemis: the Calydonian Boar, and the herds vanish from the hills;
  - Ares: the Chimera, or brawls and fires in the barracks, and soldiers desert;
  - Aphrodite: households break up in quarrels, moods fall across the city;
  - Hephaestus: Talos, or fires break out in the workshops;
  - Hermes: thieves everywhere for a season, and a caravan lost on the road;
  - Dionysus: a drunken riot (vandals across a quarter), and the wine sours in the stores.
  Each is said plainly, with what it destroyed, and the city always recovers.
- **Wrath in person:** for the greater curses the god walks in to deliver it (slice 56's figure, darker, with storm instead of light).

**The gods of Greece (slice 58)**
- **Patron gods:** Athens Athena, Sparta Ares, Corinth Poseidon, Troy Apollo, Thebes Dionysus, Argos Hera, Knossos Zeus, Rhodes Hermes. Memphis keeps its own gods. A rival city's patron favours it, and a ruler who is pious cares what your gods think.
- **Deeds in Greece move the gods:** making war on a patron's city angers that god (more without cause), and pleases Ares if won; plundering a city angers its patron a great deal; gifts sent to a city during its festival please its patron; a tribute paid angers Ares and pleases nobody; a broken promise angers Zeus and Hera; trade on every route pleases Hermes.
- **On the map of Greece:** each city's patron's emblem beside its name, and a page for Olympus in its tabs with the twelve's moods towards you (the same cards as the Olympus panel).
- **Gods abroad:** a loving god can bless an expedition (better odds, said in the report), and an angry patron makes an expedition against their city harder. Sea battles: Poseidon's mood tips them.
- **Greece's festivals** (the Panathenaia, the Karneia, the Isthmian Games) are now named for their gods and honour them.

**Heroes answer (slice 59)**
- **Seven heroes:** Heracles (any monster), Perseus (Medusa), Theseus (the Minotaur), Odysseus (the Cyclops), Bellerophon (the Chimera), Jason (Talos) and Atalanta (the Calydonian Boar).
- **A hero's hall** (4×4), one per hero, a costly building with a courtyard and the hero's statue. A hero comes only when their hall stands and their demands are met, each named with progress, from what the city already has: Heracles wants a sanctuary to Zeus, 2,000 people and a full granary; Perseus wants Athena warm and a sanctuary to her; Theseus wants a court and a city of fine houses; Odysseus wants six triremes and a sanctuary to Athena; Bellerophon wants a horse farm and twenty horsemen; Jason wants a shipyard and three trade routes; Atalanta wants Artemis warm and three hunting lodges.
- **The hero arrives** (a ship or a road, with a message), lives at the hall, walks the streets as a large figure (half again a man's height, with their attributes), and lifts the city's mood.
- **Slaying:** send a hero against a monster from the hall's panel or the monster's: they walk to its lair and fight it, a long fight you can watch, and win (a hero of that monster always wins; Heracles against any). The city celebrates for a week, the monster's god is satisfied and calms, and the lair becomes a place of legend that pleases visitors.
- **Heroes abroad:** a hero can join an expedition, raising its odds a great deal.
- **Each hero has one wish of their own** once they've come (Heracles wants a festival to Zeus each year; Odysseus wants to sail home now and then and is away a season). Ignored, they leave in time.

**Gods in war (slice 60)**
- **Your champion.** When invaders reach your map, any god who is loving and whose sanctuary stands can be called from the Army tab or the sanctuary's panel (once a year per god). They arrive with their flourish, walk to the raiders and strike: raiders near them fall or flee. Ares and Athena fight best; Zeus throws lightning from afar; Poseidon sends a wave at a landing; Apollo's arrows; Artemis's arrows; Hephaestus's fire; Aphrodite and Hermes don't fight but break the enemy's nerve. A god in your streets stays a few days.
- **Their champion.** A rival's patron god whose mood towards you is angry or furious can march with that city's army or raid. It's always said first: the threat's message and the band's chip on the map of Greece show the god with them ("Ares marches with Sparta"). On your map the god walks with the band, strikes your soldiers and burns buildings. A hero, or a god of your own who loves you, can face them: two gods fight in your streets until one withdraws (the stronger love wins). Your army alone can't hurt a god, but holding out until the band breaks sends the god home with them.
- **No god fights another god's city on the map of Greece**; the gods only tip odds there (slice 58).

**Beloved of the gods (slice 61)**
- **The fourth ambition**, chased alongside the other three (Oliver's choice from phase 5: all at once). Five milestones: a shrine to every one of the twelve; a first sanctuary finished; three gods loving at once; a monster slain by a hero; six sanctuaries standing (its great monument). Rewards as the others (gift, settlers, renown).
- **`#olympus`**: a prepared city with six sanctuaries (one rising), two gods loving and one angry with an omen running, a monster in its lair and a hero's hall waiting on one demand, a rival's band with its god on the way, and a year of messages.
- Tuning across a few years of play, the budgets, and any leftovers.

## Technical design

- **State (`src/sim/state.ts`):** one top-level field per system, with its types in its own module:
  - `gods` (groundwork, slice 53): each god's memories, a monthly mood record, festivals and offerings held, omens and curses (57), visits and figures in the city (56), the gods at war (60);
  - sanctuaries live as buildings of one new kind `sanctuary` with a `god` field, and their stages in `s.monuments.sites` with the monuments' (54);
  - `legends` (slices 55 and 59): monsters (lair, health, state), heroes (hall, demands, arrived, where), legends' places;
  - shrines gain a `god` field on the building record (53).
- **The twelve's table** (`src/sim/pantheon.ts`, groundwork): ids, names, colours, emblems, what they love and hate, rivals, sacred goods, patron cities, monsters and heroes, and the mood API: `honour(s, god, n, why, fade)`, `moodOfGod(s, god)`, `reasonsOf(s, god)`, `noticed(s)`. Every line reads and writes moods only through this API.
- **New modules:**
  - `src/sim/worship.ts` (53: dedications, festivals, offerings, what gods love read from the city), `src/sim/wrath.ts` and `blessings.ts` (57), `src/ui/olympus.ts` (53);
  - `src/sim/sanctuaries.ts` and `src/render/sanctuary.ts` (54);
  - `src/sim/monsters.ts`, `src/sim/heroes.ts`, `src/render/monsters.ts`, `src/render/heroes.ts` (55, 59);
  - `src/sim/epiphany.ts` (the gods' visits and walks) and `src/render/gods.ts` (the figures and flourishes) (56), `src/sim/godwar.ts` (60);
  - Greece's gods in `src/sim/greecegods.ts` and `src/render/greece/*` (58).
- **Messages** through `say()`; **panels** through the building-panel registry and the kit; the **overview** gets a Gods page.
- **Rendering:**
  - god figures are a few dozen low-poly parts each, with an emissive material in the god's colour, a ground halo (a transparent disc) and a trail of fading instanced sprites; at most a handful at once, so they cost little;
  - the flourishes (lightning, spray, petals, sparks) are short particle bursts from one shared pool;
  - sanctuaries share one mesh builder with per-god parts, instanced where several stand; monsters and heroes are their own small meshes;
  - curse signs (a dark cloud, a red sky, blight on fields) are tints and sprites on the existing meshes.
- **Money:** festivals, offerings, sanctuaries, halls and Zeus's gift go through the treasury with their own reasons, so the ledger invariant holds.
- **Principles held:** the sim stays pure and deterministic (hashes and seeded random only, `dmath` maths); the renderer and UI only read; every action is a command; nothing beyond the walls that the city can't feel.
- **Targets tuned by eye:**
  - how fast moods rise and fall, how much each love and hate weighs, how loud rivalry is;
  - how often curses come, and how hard;
  - a monster's health and blows against a company;
  - how long sanctuaries take with a busy masons' yard;
  - a god figure's size, glow and pace, and the flourishes;
  - the frame budget with two gods, a monster and a band on the map at once.

## Testing

Only where it earns its keep:
- **Determinism:** the same seed and commands give the same city after N ticks with a dedicated shrine, a festival, a sanctuary rising, a curse, a monster's raid, a hero's fight, a god's visit and a god in a battle.
- **Independence:** a town with no gods honoured, no monster and no hero plays exactly as before until it is noticed; every older demo opens exactly as before.
- **Saves:** a round-trip with a monster in its lair, a hero away, an omen running and a god in the streets. A version 47 save loads and plays on (its shrines to all the gods).
- **The ledger invariant** with festivals, offerings, sanctuaries, halls and Zeus's gift.
- **Wrath is always warned:** no curse lands without its omen at least a month before; an offering that lifts the mood past angry always turns it.
- **Routing:** monsters reach their targets and their lair; heroes reach the lair; a god reaches each place on their visit and leaves where they stand, their flourish reversed ("leave as they came"); the carts haul to sanctuaries.
- **Monsters never die to soldiers;** a hero of that monster always wins.
- **Budgets:** the prepared demos still open under 3 seconds; `#olympus` with two gods, a monster and a band holds the frame budget headless.
