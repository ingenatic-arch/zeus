# Phase 2: A richer city

*16 Oct 2026. Chosen by Oliver after phase 1 ("A living neighbourhood"), from its "Recorded for later" list: more goods, storehouses, taxes and wages, and the original's hazards. Serves pillar 1, "The city feels alive" ("Production chains feed storehouses and markets, as in the original"), and lays the ground for pillar 4, "A living market and the sea lanes".*

## Summary

At the end of phase 1 the town grows to fine houses, but money only piles up (about 15,000 drachmas by year four) and there is nothing to make, store or sell besides wheat. This phase gives the city an economy. Olive groves, sheep pastures, vineyards, a hunting lodge and a dairy feed storehouses and the agora. Bread, meat and cheese make a varied table. Oil, fleece and wine lift houses all the way to a new top level, the villa. Tax collectors walk the streets, every building pays its workers, and a ledger says where the money went. Fire and collapse build up where nobody looks after the streets, and you can see them coming. A merchant ship calls at the harbour to buy what the city has spare. By the end, money matters: a city can run short, and it recovers by building industries that sell.

## Claude's defaults (Oliver can change any of these)

**The chains**
- **Olive grove → olive press.** A grove (3×3) goes on the dry hillside and scrub land, where the map already has wild olives; trees grow and are picked, and carts take olives to a press, which makes jars of oil.
- **Sheep pasture → fleece.** The pasture (3×3, a fold with a shearing shed) goes on grass and scrub. A small flock grazes around it on the open ground, and the shepherd shears them into bales of fleece. One building, no second step.
- **Vineyard → winery.** A vineyard (3×3) of trellised vines goes on sunny slopes; carts take grapes to a winery, which makes amphorae of wine.
- **Hunting lodge.** Wild deer and boar roam in herds on the hills and in the woods. The lodge's hunters leave the road and walk across open country to the nearest herd, and come back with meat. Herds thin when hunted hard and slowly grow back, and the lodge says so when game is scarce nearby.
- **Dairy.** A dairy (3×3) keeps a herd of goats that graze on the open ground beside it. The dairy milks them and makes cheese, so it is one building that shows the whole step from goat to cheese.
- **Fertile land is per crop.** Wheat still wants the river land; olives and vines want dry slopes; pastures and the dairy want grass. Placement tints show how well each spot suits what you're placing.
- **Foods go to the granary, goods to a storehouse.** Meat and cheese join wheat in the granary. A new storehouse (3×3, a walled yard of jars, bales and amphorae) takes oil, fleece and wine. Carts take everything by the shortest road, as wheat carts do now. Both show what they hold on hover.
- **The agora grows stalls.** The agora gets room for four stalls: food, oil, fleece and wine. The food stall exists today; the other three are added one by one from the build bar, and each sends its own vendor walking. The food vendor's basket carries whatever mix of bread, meat and cheese the stall has.

**Houses**
- **The ladder is now:** tent (needs nothing) → hut (water) → cottage (one food) → townhouse (shrine, two foods, oil) → fine house (theatre, three foods, fleece) → villa (wine). Each step lists exactly what's missing in the household panel, as today.
- **The villa** is a new sixth model: the grandest house in town, a courtyard house with a colonnade, a tiled roof, a garden and an olive tree. It holds more people and pays the most tax.
- **Existing towns get a grace period.** When a new need appears (oil, a second food), homes that were already above it get the usual warning period before anything declines, so loading an old town doesn't drop it at once.

**Money**
- **Taxes replace rent.** A tax office (2×2) sends out a tax collector on a round like other walkers. He taxes each home beside his way at most once a month, by level and tax rate, and brings the coins back. Homes he never passes pay nothing, and the hover tip and the household panel say so ("No tax collector has called this month").
- **The tax rate** has five settings: none, light, fair (the default), heavy and harsh. It's in the top bar beside the drachmas. Heavy and harsh sour every household's mood, and the chatter grumbles about it. At harsh, fed-up families can leave. There's no other penalty, and setting it back down lifts moods again within a month.
- **Wages.** On the first of each month, every workplace pays a fixed wage per worker it actually has. There's no wage slider this phase.
- **Upkeep is just wages.** Buildings don't have a separate maintenance fee; wear (below) is the upkeep, and the inspector is the cost of keeping it down.
- **The ledger** opens from the drachmas readout. It shows this month and last month: taxes, sales and other income in; wages, building and clearing out. Income is grouped by street district and wages by building kind. The drachmas readout shows a small arrow for which way money went last month.
- **Running dry.** Below zero drachmas you can't build anything new. Wages that can't be paid go unpaid, workers at unpaid workplaces work at half speed, and their households grumble. As soon as there's money again, wages resume. There's no debt and no game over. The ledger and a top-bar note say plainly that the treasury is empty.
- **Starting money** stays 5,000. Costs, wages and tax are tuned by eye so that a phase 1 style town of about 300 people, with no goods, roughly breaks even at a fair rate. It only gets rich with villas and goods to sell.

**Fire and collapse**
- **Fire risk** rises over time in every building, faster in busy and crowded ones (presses, wineries, the theatre, packed houses). **Wear** rises in every building, faster in big ones. Both show as a small mark on the building once they're past half, and in two new overlays (fire risk and wear), with the worst buildings tinted red.
- **The watch post** (2×2) sends a watchman on a round; every building beside his way has its fire risk reset. **The architect's office** (2×2) sends an inspector; every building beside her way has its wear reset.
- **Smoulder first.** A building whose risk tops out smoulders for about a month: a thin column of smoke, a crackle in the sound, and a line in the town notes ("Smoke over the Kallias house on the river road"). A watchman passing in that time puts it out. If none does, it catches fire.
- **Fire spreads** to the buildings next to it, one at a time, unless a watchman reaches the fire. A watchman who can see flames from his round walks straight to them and fights them, so watch posts near crowded streets are the cure. A burnt building becomes rubble and its family leaves.
- **Collapse** has a similar warning: past the mark, a building creaks and sheds dust for about a month, then collapses into rubble if no inspector comes. Collapse never spreads.
- **Rubble** must be cleared (with demolish, for a small fee) before you can rebuild on it. The ledger lists what fire and collapse cost.
- **Crime** waits for a later phase.

**The trading post**
- **The trading post** (3×3, a stone quay with a crane and a counting house) is built on the bay's shore, on land touching deep water, and needs a road. Only one is allowed.
- **The ship is telegraphed.** A merchant ship from overseas calls every three months. The post and the top bar show how many days until she's due, and a sail appears on the horizon a few days before she arrives.
- **Choosing what to sell.** The post's panel has a row per good (wheat, meat, cheese, oil, fleece and wine), each with sell on or off and how much to keep for the city. In the weeks before she's due, porters carry the surplus from the granary and storehouse to the quay, where it stacks up visibly.
- **The visit.** The ship sails into the bay, ties up, loads what's on the quay at the prices shown on the post, pays, and sails off. The coins arrive in the ledger as sales, by good.
- **Steady prices** this phase, with fine goods (wine and oil) worth most and wheat least. No buying from the ship yet. Moving prices, buying, rival cities and sea lanes come with the wider world.

**Saves and other things**
- **Old saves load.** Phase 1 saves are migrated: rent becomes taxes, and the town starts with no tax office, so a note says to build one. Every building starts with no fire risk or wear. New saves are a new version.
- **Saves get smaller** (packed arrays rather than plain lists), because the state grows a lot this phase. There are still two slots.
- **The new walkers** (tax collector, watchman, inspector, hunters, shepherds and the four vendors) and animals (goats, sheep, deer and boar) are built in code in the same toy style. The vendors carry what they sell: a basket, oil jars, a bale or a wine jug.
- **The new overlays** are the four goods (oil, fleece and wine coverage, and food variety), tax collection, fire risk and wear.
- **Demo links** are bare hash tokens, one or more per slice, listed in each check-in.
- **Phase 1's small rough edges are fixed in the last slice:**
  - "Back to a hut" notes stack when a whole street falls back;
  - two shortfall lines can swap places;
  - starting a new town replaces the old town's autosave.

## What's there today

Phase 1 is complete (slices 1–7). There's a hand-shaped coast, roads, bridges and housing plots, and settlers and leavers. One food chain runs from wheat farm to ox carts to granary to the agora's food stall and vendor. Fountains, a shrine and a theatre each send a walker on a round. There are five house levels, households with names, moods and chatter, warnings then decline, overlays, two save slots, sound and a 300-person demo town.

- **Money:** rent is collected automatically each month (1 to 9 drachmas a household, by level), and the only cost is building. Nothing costs anything to run, so money only grows.
- **Goods and storage:** there is one good (wheat, in measures) and no storehouse. The agora holds one stall.
- **Hazards and the coast:** nothing ages, burns or breaks, and the bay is only scenery.
- **The code:** buildings are a closed list of seven kinds (`src/sim/state.ts`), and the round logic (`src/sim/rounds.ts`) is already shared by every service walker. Both make the new walkers and buildings mostly a matter of adding data. The state is at version 6, and saves are about 450 KB.

He adds an oil stall to the agora, and its vendor walks out with jars on a yoke. Townhouses with oil keep their level; ones without warn. Sheep on the hillside give fleece for fine houses. **Fire and collapse.** Neglected corners of the town start to show it: a red mark on a crowded block, then a thread of smoke and a note in the town lines. Porters stack bales and jars on the quay; a sail appears on the horizon; the ship comes into the bay, loads and sails off, and the ledger shows the sale. A town that was running short is back in the black.

**A richer town.** By the end, a prepared demo town of a few hundred people shows every chain at work, villas on the best streets, watchmen and inspectors on their rounds and a ship in the bay, and he can judge how it all looks and whether money feels tight in a good way.

## Technical design

- **State (`src/sim/state.ts`):**
  - new building kinds: grove, press, pasture, vineyard, winery, lodge, dairy, storehouse, tax office, watch post, architect's office and trading post, plus agora stalls as a part of the agora;
  - goods as a fixed list with per-building stock;
  - homes get per-food and per-good stocks;
  - per-building fire risk and wear;
  - rubble as a tile or building state;
  - wild herds and grazing animals as light entities;
  - the treasury's monthly ledger and the ship's schedule;
  - the state version is bumped, with a migration from version 6.
- **Production:** one shared model for "a workplace that turns time and workers (and, for presses and wineries, an input) into stock". Wheat moves onto it, and its carts deliver to the nearest store with room for that good. Food (`src/sim/food.ts`) becomes foods; the storehouse is the granary's sibling.
- **Walkers:**
  - tax collector, watchman, inspector and the three new vendors use the shared round logic;
  - porters for the agora stalls and the trading post use shortest paths as now;
  - hunters, the shepherd's flock, goats and wild herds move off-road on the terrain with a simple seeded wander and go-to;
  - a watchman's dash to a fire is a shortest path.
- **Fire:** deterministic risk and wear counters; smoulder, burn and spread on a fixed schedule with seeded chance; spread checks the four neighbours.
- **Money:** every change to the treasury goes through one function with a reason, which fills the ledger, so the ledger always adds up.
- **Rendering:** the new buildings, villa, walkers, animals, stalls, smoke, flames, rubble and ship are built in code. Settled buildings are merged per chunk, and walkers and animals are instanced, as in phase 1. Fire and smoke are cheap particles, capped.
- **UI:** ledger panel, tax rate control, trading post panel, the new overlays, and storehouse and granary hover tips.
- **Targets tuned by eye:**
  - wages, tax per level per rate, and costs;
  - fire risk and wear rates, smoulder and creak times, and spread chance;
  - herd sizes and regrowth, and production rates;
  - ship interval and prices;
  - how much each house level eats and uses.

## Testing

Only where it earns its keep:
- determinism: the same seed and commands give the same city after N ticks, now with fire, herds and the ship in it;
- save and load round-trip to the same state, and a phase 1 (version 6) save loads and plays on;
- the ledger invariant: over any run, the sum of the ledger's lines equals the change in the treasury;
- routing: carts deliver each good to the right kind of store, and porters fetch from the nearest one that has it;
- fire: a fire with no watchman spreads by the rules; one with a watchman on the street is put out;
- budgets: the prepared demo town holds the frame-rate and load targets, and the save size is measured.
