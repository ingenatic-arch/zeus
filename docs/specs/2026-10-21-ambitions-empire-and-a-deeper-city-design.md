# Phase 5: Ambitions, empire and a deeper city

*21 Oct 2026. Chosen by Oliver after phase 4 ("A city worth defending"): two of the three offered phases in one, "Ambitions and empire" and "A deeper city", built with "parallel development in mind to reduce development time where possible, without sacrificing scope". The gods are not in this phase. It serves pillar 3, "A world that remembers" ("You can found colonies or bring rivals under your rule, and run them lightly: set what they send you and watch their loyalty"); the vision's **Ambitions** ("you choose a path, monuments and milestones mark your progress, and you can keep playing once you reach it"); and pillar 1, "The city feels alive" ("Each house is a household with needs, a mood and a small story"), through crime, seasons and grand estates. It also clears the leftovers recorded in phases 2 to 4. Pillar 1 still wins every conflict: every vassal, colony and monument must show up as something that changes in the city.*

## Summary

At the end of phase 4 the city can trade, borrow, lend and defend itself, but it has nothing to play towards, and Greece stands still around it: the same six cities, the same size, for ever. Its streets are calm in a way real cities aren't, with no crime, no seasons, and no homes grander than a villa. This phase gives the game a direction and the city more depth.

Three **ambitions** run side by side from the founding (Master of Greece, The great trading city and A city of wonders), and you chase whichever you like. Milestones mark your way along each, and each ambition ends in a great monument. **Greece grows**: rival cities rise and fall in population, wealth and strength, more cities and empty sites appear on the map, and rivals found colonies and take vassals of their own. You can bring rivals under your rule by conquest, by awe of your army, or through their debts to you, and you can found **colonies** by shipping settlers out. Both are run lightly: you set what they send and watch their loyalty, and tribute arrives in your stores. **Monuments** rise in stages from marble, timber and bronze that you quarry, fell and haul. **The seas are contested**: blockades, enemy fleets taking cargo, pirates meeting expeditions. Inside the walls, **crime** grows where poverty sits next to wealth and the watch walks rarely. **The year turns**, and the land, the harvests and the sailing season change with it. **Grand estates** house an aristocracy that wants everything, pays the most, and rides out as horsemen.

The phase is planned to be **built on three lines at once** (see *The parallel plan*), so eleven slices take about five slices' worth of time.

## Claude's defaults (Oliver can change any of these)

**Ambitions (slice 39)**
- **Three ambitions to start:** *Master of Greece* (vassals and colonies, an army feared across Greece), *The great trading city* (routes, trade, the richest treasury) and *A city of wonders* (monuments, grand estates, a great population). *Beloved of the gods* comes with the gods phase.
- **All three at once (Oliver's choice).** There is nothing to pick: every town works toward all three from its founding, and whatever you do counts toward one of them.
- **Milestones.** Each ambition has five, from easy to grand, and each one names exactly what it wants and how far you are ("2 of 3 vassals: Sparta, Thebes"). Reaching one brings a town line, a short fanfare, a laurel on the panel and a small reward (a ruler's gift, settlers drawn by fame, or the right to build the next monument). The last is the ambition's own great monument: the Colossus for Master of Greece, the Lighthouse for The great trading city, the Stadium for A city of wonders.
- **After the end** you keep playing: that ambition's column says "Achieved in year 9", and the other two carry on. Achieving all three is the game's grand finish, with a town line from every ruler.
- **Where you see it:** a laurel chip in the top bar (the nearest milestone across all three and how close), and an Ambition panel from it with the three side by side.

**Greece grows (slice 31)**
- **More cities.** Thebes and Argos inland, Knossos on Crete, Rhodes, and Memphis in Egypt at the edge of the map, each with a ruler, traits, goods and wants, in the shuffle like the first four. Five to seven **empty sites** (islands and capes such as Melos, Thasos, Naxos, Kythera, Cyrene) are marked as places a city could be founded, each with what it would make (marble on Naxos, timber on Thasos, copper and so on).
- **Cities grow and shrink.** Every city has a population, wealth, an army and a fleet that change with its harvests, its trade (including yours), its wars and its debts. The map shows it: the model of the city grows, its label gets a size, and its panel says why it changed ("grew: good harvests, trade with you"; "shrank: lost a war with Athens").
- **Rivals reach too.** A strong rival founds colonies on empty sites and can make a weaker neighbour its vassal, with a line in the News of Greece. A site can be taken before you get there, so the map is a race.
- **Raids** come about twice as often as in phase 4 (balance card), still always telegraphed with a threat and a deadline.
- **Leftovers:** Troy's label under the lines at 1600 × 900; a caravan in your streets stops trading when its road is closed; a refused band no longer camps for 70 days in `#threat`; an offer's yearly limit is shared across a land and a sea route to the same city; price arrows coloured for what the move means to you (good or bad), not only its direction.

**Vassals and allies (slice 34)**
- **Three ways to take a city (Oliver's choice).**
  - *Conquest:* send a full force (the phase 4 expedition) against a city with the new aim "to conquer". It marches or sails, meets the city's army, and comes back with a report; a win makes the city your vassal.
  - *Awe:* a cold or wary city whose army is much smaller than yours can be sent a "submit to us" demand. It may bow (vassal), refuse (a grudge), or ask allies for help. The odds are shown with reasons before you send it ("likely: their army 40 against your 180; Sparta might come to their aid").
  - *Debt:* a city that owes you more than it can repay can be offered "your debt for your loyalty" in place of payment.
- **Run lightly.** A vassal's panel has its tribute (coin, or one of its goods, light, fair or heavy each year) and its loyalty with every reason (the weight of the tribute, your army's strength, gifts, time under you, others' whispers). Tribute arrives by caravan or ship and lands in your stores and treasury, with its own ledger and stock book lines. A vassal sends a company of its men when you are raided (its men walk in from its road), and won't trade against you.
- **Rebellion is telegraphed.** Falling loyalty is said aloud a season ahead ("Corinth is arming; loyalty low: heavy tribute, your army abroad"). You can lighten the tribute, send a gift or march. A rebel city is a rival again, with a grudge.
- **Allies.** A warm ruler can be offered an alliance: each comes to the other's aid against raids and wars (their men arrive on your road, yours march on the map), and each asks the other to join its wars, remembered if refused. This is the recorded "alliances and joining rivals' wars".
- **Lenders' armies** come back only when what is still owed is worth the march (balance card).

**Colonies (slice 37)**
- **Founding.** On an empty site's panel: "Found a colony" with its price (drachmas, a few households of settlers, and starting goods). The settlers are real families from your town: they pack, walk to the quay with their goods and board a ship that sails out across the map. Their homes stand empty behind them.
- **The colony** appears on the map as a small town that grows by itself over years. It has a name you can change and a governor with one trait. It makes its site's good and sends you a share you choose (as with vassals' tribute, by ship), and its loyalty has reasons (how much you take, whether you protect it, how long it has been).
- **Threats.** Rivals can raid or claim a colony, telegraphed on the map; you answer with triremes or an expedition, or let it fall. A colony that grows large and is squeezed may declare itself free.

**Marble and timber (slice 32)**
- **The quarry** (new, 3×3) goes on rock with marble in it (pale outcrops on the coast, shown while placing it). Quarrymen cut blocks that carters take to the storehouse on sledges.
- **The loggers' camp** (new, 2×2) goes at the edge of a wood. Loggers fell trees you can see go down, and the wood grows back slowly (so a camp can strip a hillside if you let it). Timber goes to the storehouse.
- **From abroad:** Athens and later Naxos sell marble; Troy and Thasos sell timber.
- **Leftovers:** miners are drawn at the copper mine with picks and carts; the caravan yard's blank wall on a slope.

**Monuments (slice 35)**
- **Three monuments:** the **Lighthouse** on a headland by the harbour (ships come and go faster, pirates keep clear of your waters), the **Colossus** at the harbour mouth (every ruler's mood is warmer, "they speak of your Colossus"), and the **Stadium** (games every four years: visitors, trade, a festival mood across the city).
- **Built in stages you can watch:** a marked-out site, then foundations, then columns or the bronze frame, then the roof or the finish. Each stage needs marble, timber and (for the Colossus) bronze hauled in by porters with oxen carts from the storehouses, and builders from a new **masons' yard** (2×2). A stage starts only when its materials are on site, and the site says what it waits for ("Columns: 40 of 120 marble on site; 6 builders of 12").
- **A monument takes a year or two** of a busy town and is the most expensive thing in the game; you can have one of each. Rulers notice when one is finished.

**Contested seas (slice 38)**
- **Blockades.** A city at war with you can send a fleet to sit off your harbour: the trading ship and sea routes can't come or go until your triremes break it or the war ends. It is shown on the map and in the bay days ahead.
- **Wars at sea take cargo:** enemy fleets in a war take cargoes on that city's lanes, not only pirates.
- **Expeditions at sea** can meet pirates, and their triremes fight them.
- **Sea fights wound ships:** oars break, and a crippled trireme limps home to the shipyard for repairs.
- **The shipyard gets its own panel:** build, crew, repair, and each ship's state.
- **Leftovers:** a band from the sea lands from the ship nearest its target, near where it is going; collectors from across the sea take about a fortnight from their sails to the beach, not 24 days; the merchant ship's way in is quicker (phase 2's question).

**Crime (slice 30)**
- **Where it comes from.** Every home has a temptation to crime that rises with want (needs unmet, hunger), unhappiness, harsh taxes and envy (richer homes within a few tiles) and falls with the watch walking by. A home's panel names it ("Tempted to steal: hungry, villas across the road, no watchman this month").
- **Thieves.** When it builds up, a thief (a hooded figure) slips out of the home and walks to a richer home (coin), a store (goods) or the tax office (the treasury), takes something and slips back. The robbed home says so ("Robbed last month") and its mood falls; the ledger has a "Stolen" line and the stock book a "stolen" one.
- **The watch.** The watch post's watchman, who already fights fire, also deters crime along his round, and chases and catches a thief he meets; the thief's family pays a fine. The town line says "A thief caught on the Street of Potters".
- **A loaded town** starts with no temptation anywhere, building up from loading, as fire did; prepared histories are played with crime off.
- **Leftovers:** early fire builds a little slower in a town's first year, and warns sooner; harsh taxes bite harder (more households leave, and crime rises); tax money comes in smoothly (each collector's takings reach the treasury day by day); marked-out plots with no road show a clear "no road" sign.

**The turning year (slice 33)**
- **Four seasons** a year (the game's year keeps its length). The land goes from green spring through gold summer to an amber autumn and a grey-green, rainy winter; trees, fields and the sea's colour follow, with rain and the odd storm.
- **Rhythm (Oliver's choice, look and rhythm):** crops grow through spring and summer and are reaped in autumn (a farm's year has a shape, and granaries fill in autumn); herds breed in spring; olives and grapes are picked in autumn; winter storms slow ships and keep most armies home (they still can come, more rarely). A season dial in the top bar says what's coming.
- **Leftover:** the camera no longer ends up inside trees on hillsides.

**The great houses (slice 36)**
- **The estate** (new, 4×4, its own plot tool): a walled house with a courtyard, garden and stable. It grows in two levels, *estate* and *great estate*, and needs what a villa needs plus marble for its statues, armour for its sons and horses.
- **Horses** come from a new **horse farm** (3×3) on grassland, or from Thessaly by caravan through Thebes.
- **Horsemen.** Each estate can send one son to ride in a company of horsemen (fast, good against archers and fleeing raiders, weak against a hoplite line). This is the recorded "chariots and horsemen"; chariots stay for later.
- **Estates pay the most tax** and draw envy (crime nearby rises).

**An empire's city (slice 40)**
- `#empire`: a prepared town with an ambition well under way, a vassal, a colony, a monument rising, two estates, winter coming, a thief on the loose and a blockade threatened, for judging it all together and checking the budgets.
- Numbers tuned across a few years of play; a look-and-feel pass over everything new.

## Technical design

- **State (`src/sim/state.ts`):** each new system adds **one top-level field** with its types in its own module, so the shared state file changes by a few lines per slice:
  - `crime` (slice 30): per-home temptation and robbed marks live on the home record; the field holds thieves' clock and the month's tallies;
  - `world` grows (slice 31): cities become data (the fixed `CityId` union becomes a string id with a table), with size, wealth, army and fleet per city, sites, colonies and vassal links between cities;
  - `materials` and `monuments` (slices 32 and 35): quarry and loggers' state, the regrowth of felled trees, monument sites and their stages;
  - `season` (slice 33): derived from the tick where possible, so it is cheap and never drifts;
  - `empire` (slices 34 and 37): your vassals, allies and colonies, tribute settings, loyalty and their reasons;
  - `fleet` grows (slice 38): blockades, ship damage;
  - estates (slice 36) are homes with two new levels; horses a new ware;
  - `ambition` (slice 39): the progress toward each of the three and the milestones reached, with when.
- **New modules:** `src/sim/crime.ts`, `seasons.ts`, `estates.ts` (city line); `src/sim/growth.ts`, `vassals.ts`, `colonies.ts` (Greece line); `src/sim/materials.ts`, `monuments.ts` (works line); `src/sim/ambitions.ts`; each with a matching `src/ui/` panel and, where drawn, a `src/render/` module.
- **Rendering:** thieves, quarrymen, loggers, carts with oxen, colonists and horsemen as instanced figures joining the existing batches; a felled-tree state in the trees; monument stages as meshes that build up; the estate and great estate; season tints applied through the city look's palette (`src/render/finish.ts`) and the trees' colours, not new meshes; on the map of Greece, cities that change size, sites, colonies and vassal banners, and blockading fleets.
- **Money:** tribute, fines, theft, colonies' costs, monument building and estates' taxes go through the treasury with their own reasons, so the ledger invariant holds.
- **Principles held:** the sim stays pure and deterministic (seeded random only, `dmath` maths); the renderer and UI only read; every action is a command; nothing on the map the city can't feel.
- **Targets tuned by eye:** how fast temptation builds and how often thieves go out; the season's colours and harvest timing; growth rates of rival cities; the odds of awe and conquest; tribute sizes and loyalty drift; colony growth and its share; quarry and loggers' pace and tree regrowth; monument costs and stage times; estates' needs and taxes; horsemen's strength; how often blockades come; milestone thresholds.

## The parallel plan

Oliver asked for the phase to be built with parallel work wherever it saves time without dropping scope. The phase splits into three lines that touch different parts of the code, and each line's slices come one after another, as before. The lines run at the same time, each in its own copy of the repository (a git worktree) on its own branch.

| Line | Branch | Owns (mostly) | Slices |
|---|---|---|---|
| **City** | `claude/p5-city` | homes, people, farms and herds, fire, taxes; the land's look | 30 Crime, 33 The turning year, 36 The great houses |
| **Greece** | `claude/p5-greece` | the map of Greece, rulers, routes, raids, loans, expeditions | 31 Greece grows, 34 Vassals and allies, 37 Colonies |
| **Works and sea** | `claude/p5-works` | new production, monuments, the fleet and the sea lanes | 32 Marble and timber, 35 Monuments, 38 Contested seas |
| **Trunk** | `claude/project-thread-d84eqt` (and `main`) | merges, then the slices that need all three | 39 Ambitions, 40 An empire's city |

**Waves and what waits for what**

```
wave 1      wave 2          wave 3                  then
30 Crime ── 33 Seasons ──── 36 Great houses ─┐
31 Greece ─ 34 Vassals ──── 37 Colonies ─────┼──── 40 An empire's city ── phase review
32 Marble ─ 35 Monuments ── 38 Contested seas┤
              └──(after wave 2 merges)─ 39 Ambitions ─┘
```

- Within a line, each slice builds on the one before (crime before seasons because both change homes; vassals before colonies because colonies reuse loyalty and tribute; marble before monuments).
- Across lines, the only dependencies point backwards in time, so no line waits for another mid-wave:
  - 36 (estates) needs marble from 32 (merged in wave 1);
  - 37 (colonies) and 38 (contested seas) read the seasons from 33 (merged in wave 2) for the sailing season;
  - 38 reads the new cities from 31 (wave 1);
  - 39 (ambitions) needs vassals (34) and monuments (35) from wave 2, and starts as soon as wave 2 is merged, running alongside wave 3 as a fourth line. Its milestones for colonies (37) and estates (36) are wired in when it merges, last of wave 3.
- **Wave 1 depends on no open question**, so it starts while Oliver answers the cards (seasons comes in wave 2 for this reason).
- 40 and the phase review need everything, so they run on the trunk alone.
- Eleven slices in five slice-lengths of time (three waves, then 40, then the review), against eleven one after another.

**How a slice reaches the trunk**
1. The builder and the reviewer work on the line's branch, in the line's worktree, and push the line's branch after every commit (never `main`, never another line's branch).
2. When the reviewer is done, the orchestrator merges the line into the trunk (`git merge --no-ff`, never rebase or force-push), in the order the slices finish. It resolves the shared files by the rules below, renumbers the state version, and runs the invariants on the merged trunk (typecheck, the full tests, the build and a smoke of the new demos and a few old ones). Then it pushes the trunk and `main`, and syncs the shared mirror.
3. Before a line starts its next slice, it merges the trunk back into its branch, so every slice starts from everything merged so far and measures its *Before* there.

**State versions: numbered at merge, not at start.** Phase 4's two parallel lines both took the next version and collided. Now:
- A slice's migration is written as its own function, `fromSliceN`, which only fills in its own new fields (if absent) and never assumes another line's fields exist. Two lines' migrations then work in either order.
- On its line, a slice takes trunk's version plus one, as usual, so the line's tests and saves work.
- At merge the integrator gives the merged slice the next free version on the trunk: it sets `SIM_STATE_VERSION`, renames the `if (st.version === N)` line in `migrate.ts`, and adds the slice's sentence to the migration notes. The first slice to merge gets 28, the next 29, and so on. Saves made on a line branch before its merge are throwaway.
- Tests compare with `SIM_STATE_VERSION`, never a literal, except the tests of a migration step itself.

**Shared numbering and lists: append only, trunk first.** Saves store kinds by their position in a list, and wares are numbered, so the lines agree up front:
- **Wares** are reserved now: marble 10 and timber 11 (works line), horses 12 (city line). `GOOD_COUNT`, `WARES` and `WARE_NAMES` end up with all three. Each line adds only its own, and a migration pads goods arrays to whatever `GOOD_COUNT` is (never to a fixed length), so it works whichever merges first.
- **Building, walker and round kinds** are only ever appended at the end of `BUILDING_KINDS`, `WALKER_KINDS` and `ROUND_KINDS`. At merge the trunk's entries come first and the incoming line's go after them, whatever the line had locally.
- **City ids:** slice 31 turns the fixed city list into data. The other lines keep using the existing ids until they merge the trunk back.

**Shared files and who touches them**

| File | Rule |
|---|---|
| `src/sim/state.ts` | one new top-level field per system, plus new kinds appended at the end of the lists |
| `src/sim/migrate.ts` | one `fromSliceN` function and one line per slice; renumbered at merge |
| `src/sim/sim.ts` | one step call per new system: city systems after `stepHazards`, Greece's after `stepDiplomacy`, the works' after `stepMakers` |
| `src/sim/commands.ts`, `src/game/orders.ts` | new command kinds appended |
| `src/sim/foods.ts`, `src/sim/stocks.ts` | only the reserved wares and their `WARE_INFO` rows |
| `src/game/demos.ts`, `tests/prepared.ts`, `scripts/smoke.mjs`, `scripts/measure.mjs` | new demos appended in a block headed by the slice number |
| `src/ui/categories.ts`, `src/ui/sidepanel.ts`, `src/ui/topbar.ts`, `src/ui/help.ts` | new rows appended in the slice's own block |
| `docs/CHECKINS.md` | each check-in at the top; the integrator keeps them newest first in merge order |

Any other change outside a line's own files (for example slice 38 teaching `expeditions.ts`, a Greece-line file, to meet pirates) is kept small and listed in the plan's *As built* under **Touched outside the line**, so the integrator looks at it closely at merge.

**Histories and the "nothing else changed" check.** Each slice checks that earlier demos open as before against its own starting point. Some slices change histories on purpose, and say so in their check-in: 31 (Greece grows, so every town with routes trades with a changing Greece), 33 (seasons move every harvest) and 38 (wars at sea take cargo). Crime starts at zero when a town opens and is played off in prepared histories, as fire was. At each merge the integrator rebuilds the prepared towns and checks that the only differences are the merged slice's own.

**Agents.** Each line has one builder at a time, followed by a reviewer, as in earlier phases. So at most four builders run at once (three in waves 1 and 2, four in wave 3 with ambitions). Briefs live in the scratchpad and are backed up to `/mnt/project-files/zeus-briefs/`.

## Testing

Only where it earns its keep:
- determinism: the same seed and commands give the same city and the same Greece after N ticks, with a theft, a harvest, a vassal's tribute, a colony founded and a monument stage built in it;
- independence: a town that builds none of the new things and conquers nothing plays exactly as before, apart from the slices that change histories on purpose;
- save and load round-trip with a colony at sea, a monument half built and a thief walking; a version 27 save loads and plays on; migrations applied in either order between lines give the same state;
- the ledger invariant with tribute, theft, fines, colonies and monuments;
- routing: thieves reach their target and get home or get caught; carts reach monument sites; settlers reach the quay;
- Greece: growth stays in bounds over 30 years (no city to zero or infinity); a rival's colony and a rival's vassal each happen in a long run;
- empire: each of the three ways to take a city does what it says; loyalty falls with heavy tribute and rises with gifts; a rebellion always gives its warning first;
- budgets: the prepared demos still open under 3 seconds; `#empire` holds the frame budget headless.

### Slice 30 — Thieves in the night (city line, wave 1)
- Temptation to crime per home (want, unhappiness, harsh taxes, envy, less with the watch), named in the home's panel.
- Thieves who walk to a richer home, a store or the tax office, steal and slip home; the robbed home's mood and line; "Stolen" in the ledger and stock book.
- The watchman deters and catches thieves along his round, with a fine and a town line.
- Leftovers: early fire gentler in the first year with sooner warnings; harsh taxes bite harder; tax money comes in day by day; a "no road" sign on unreachable plots.

*Oliver can:* open `#thieves`, watch a thief slip out of a hungry cottage beside the villas and rob one, then build a watch post and watch the next one caught; open `#nowatch` and see temptation named in a poor home's panel.

### Slice 31 — Greece grows (Greece line, wave 1)
- Cities as data; Thebes, Argos, Knossos, Rhodes and Memphis join the shuffle; empty sites marked on the map with what they'd make.
- Population, wealth, army and fleet per city, growing and shrinking with harvests, trade, wars and debts, shown on the map and in the city panel with reasons.
- Rivals found colonies on empty sites and take weaker neighbours as vassals, with News of Greece lines.
- Raids about twice as often (balance card). Leftovers: Troy's label, caravans after a road closes, the band camping in `#threat`, offer limits across a road and a sea route, price arrows by meaning.

*Oliver can:* open `#greecegrows` (a town 10 years on), see which rivals rose and fell and why, find a site Rhodes has already taken, and meet Knossos and Memphis.

### Slice 32 — Marble and timber (works line, wave 1)
- The quarry on marble rock, with quarrymen and sledges; the loggers' camp at a wood's edge, trees that fall and grow back; both to the storehouse.
- Marble and timber in the stock book, the side panel and on the routes (Athens marble, Troy timber).
- Leftovers: miners drawn at the copper mine; the caravan yard's wall on a slope.

*Oliver can:* open `#quarry`, watch blocks cut and dragged to the storehouse; open `#loggers`, watch a hillside thinned and slowly grow back.

### Slice 33 — The turning year (city line, wave 2)
- Four seasons, the land's colour, the trees, the sea and the weather following them; a season dial.
- Look and rhythm (Oliver's choice): crops reaped in autumn, herds breeding in spring, olives and grapes picked in autumn, winter storms slowing ships and keeping most armies home.
- Leftover: the camera kept out of trees on hillsides.

*Oliver can:* open `#seasons` and play a year at speed, watching the fields go green to gold and the granaries fill in autumn; open `#winter` to see the grey bay and a late ship.

### Slice 34 — Vassals and allies (Greece line, wave 2)
- Conquest by expedition, awe by demand (with odds and reasons), and debt for loyalty.
- The vassal panel: tribute and loyalty with reasons; tribute arriving by caravan or ship into the stores; vassals sending men when you are raided; rebellion telegraphed.
- Alliances with warm rulers: aid both ways and calls to join wars. Lenders' armies only for a remainder worth the march (balance card).

*Oliver can:* open `#vassal` and set Corinth's tribute, watching its caravan arrive; open `#submit` to demand Argos bow and read the odds; open `#conquer` to read a conquest report; open `#rebellion` to see loyalty fall and act on it.

### Slice 35 — Monuments (works line, wave 2)
- The masons' yard; monument sites in stages that wait for marble, timber and bronze hauled by oxen carts, and for builders.
- The Lighthouse, the Colossus and the Stadium, each with its effect in the city and in Greece.

*Oliver can:* open `#monument` to see the Lighthouse half built with carts on the road, and `#lit` to see it finished and the ship come in faster.

### Slice 36 — The great houses (city line, wave 3)
- The estate plot, its two levels and their needs (marble, armour, horses on top of a villa's); the horse farm; horses from Thessaly through Thebes.
- Companies of horsemen from estates; estates' taxes and the envy they draw.

*Oliver can:* open `#estates` to see two great estates on the hill and their stables; raise a company of horsemen and ride them at a band in `#horsemen`.

### Slice 37 — Colonies (Greece line, wave 3)
- Founding a colony on an empty site: settler families leave their homes for the quay and sail; the voyage on the map.
- The colony grows, makes its site's good and sends your share by ship; loyalty with reasons; rivals' threats to it and your answer; independence if squeezed.

*Oliver can:* open `#colony` to watch families board the ship for Thasos, and `#colonies` to see two grown colonies sending timber and marble.

### Slice 38 — Contested seas (works line, wave 3)
- Blockades of your harbour, telegraphed; enemy fleets in wars taking cargo; pirates meeting expeditions at sea; broken oars and repairs; the shipyard panel.
- Leftovers: landings from the ship nearest the target; quicker collectors by sea; the merchant ship's pace.

*Oliver can:* open `#blockade` to see Troy's fleet across the harbour mouth and break it with triremes; open `#shipyard` for the new panel and a damaged ship under repair.

### Slice 39 — Ambitions (fourth line in wave 3, after wave 2 merges)
- The laurel chip and the Ambition panel with all three ambitions side by side, counting from the founding.
- Five milestones per ambition, each with what it wants and how far you are, their rewards, and the great monument at the end; playing on after.
- Colony and estate milestones wired in at merge.

*Oliver can:* found a new town and see all three paths start; open `#ambition` to see a town a few milestones in across the three, and `#achieved` to see Master of Greece completed with the Colossus standing.

### Slice 40 — An empire's city (trunk, after everything merges)
- `#empire`: the ambitions well under way, a vassal, a colony, a monument rising, estates, a thief loose, winter coming and a blockade threatened.
- Numbers tuned across a few years of play; a look-and-feel pass over everything new; any leftovers not yet done.

*Oliver can:* open `#empire`, play it for a few years, and judge whether the city and the empire feel like one game.
