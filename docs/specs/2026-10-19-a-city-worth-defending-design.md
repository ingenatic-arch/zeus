# Phase 4: A city worth defending

Serves pillar 5, "War on land and sea" ("Invaders march onto your city map, and you command formations through your own streets and walls in real time. How you built decides how you defend"; "Expeditions abroad play out on the world map with a clear report"; "triremes guard trade routes"; "enemy fleets can land troops on your shore, and you fight off the landing yourself"), and pillar 3, "A world that remembers", through loans and debts between cities. Pillar 1 still wins every conflict: every soldier, wall and debt must show up as something that changes in the city.*

## Summary

At the end of phase 3 Greece presses on the city (traders, prices, rulers who remember, raiders who burn a field), but the city can't push back: raiders can only be bought off, and money can only be earned. This phase gives the city teeth and credit. Your best homes send their men to a barracks, armed with bronze from a new mine and armoury, and they form companies under banners that you drag through your own streets to meet raiders. Walls you draw like roads, gates where they cross a road, and towers whose archers shoot on their own decide where a fight happens. Raiders now come in force by land and from ships on your shore, loot your stores and treasury, set fires on their way and leave; your soldiers can stop them, and triremes from a shipyard can sink their ships in the bay and guard your sea lanes from pirates. Loans work both ways: you can borrow from a ruler or lend to one, cities offer and ask for loans, terms are haggled by mood, and a debt not paid brings escalating pressure, whether it is yours (a warning, then seized goods, then an army at your gates) or theirs (forgive it, demand it, or send a raiding party or a full army across the map to collect, with a clear report of what came back).

## Claude's defaults (Oliver can change any of these)

**Bronze and armour**
- **The copper mine** (new, 2×2): goes only on rocky hillside with copper in it. The coast has a few such outcrops, shown with a green-streaked rock tint while you place it. Miners with picks and a cart dig copper ore, which carters take to the storehouse, as with olives.
- **The armoury** (new, 3×3): a smithy with a forge, bellows and a rack of shields and helmets. Its smiths turn copper into armour (a helmet, a breastplate, a round shield and a spear: one set per hoplite). Armour is stored in storehouses like any good.
- **From abroad.** Corinth and Athens sell armour, and Sparta sells copper, on their routes (from the phase 3 trade panel), so a town without copper can still arm its men. Copper and armour join the stock book and the side panel's Goods tab.

**The barracks and the companies**
- **The barracks** (new, 4×4): a walled yard with a colonnade, racks of spears, a drill ground and a banner pole. It needs a road and a few staff. Up to four companies per barracks, and you can build more barracks.
- **Who serves.** Hoplites (heavy infantry with spear and shield) come from fine houses and villas, one man per home, and each needs a set of armour from the stores. Archers (light, with a bow) come from townhouses and better, one man per home, and need nothing from the stores. A man who joins leaves his job, so soldiers cost labour as well as pay. Clicking a home says "Their eldest serves in the 2nd company"; clicking a soldier says whose son he is.
- **Forming a company.** From the barracks panel: "Raise a company of hoplites" or "of archers". Men walk in from their homes over the following days, collect armour (carried from the storehouse by porters), and the company fills up to 16. A company that can't fill says why ("Only 9 fine houses and villas can spare a man", "No armour in the stores").
- **Pay.** Soldiers are paid monthly like workers, on their own ledger line ("Soldiers' pay"). Dismissing a company sends the men home to their old jobs and returns their armour to the stores.
- **Hirelings.** In a pinch, the barracks can hire a company of mercenaries (Cretan archers or Arcadian hoplites) for a hiring fee and three times a citizen's pay. They walk in from the map's edge within a few days, wear their own colours, take no men from your homes, and leave (grumbling, with a town line) if their pay runs out for a month.
- **At peace** companies drill on the barracks ground: the figures form ranks, turn and march, so a barracks looks alive.

**Commanding**
- **Banners.** Each company has a banner (its number and the city's colour, a different emblem per company). Click a banner, or a soldier, or the company's row on a new **Army tab** in the side panel, to select it; then click (or drag the banner to) a tile and it marches there by the shortest way (roads and open ground, around buildings and through gates) and holds. The company stands in ranks facing the way it marched.
- **Holding and fighting.** A company fights any enemy that comes within a few tiles of its banner, then returns to its banner. Archers shoot from about eight tiles and pull back from melee; hoplites close in. There is no other order to learn: where you plant the banner is the decision. "Back to barracks" sends it home.
- **The Army tab** lists each company: kind, men (16/16), wounded, where it is and what it is doing, its pay; and the city's whole strength. The top bar gets a small army chip when you have soldiers.
- **Pause works for orders.** You can pause, plant every banner, and play.

**Battles in the streets**
- **Who fights.** Your companies, your towers and your hirelings against raiders and landing parties. Each figure has strength, armour and health underneath; hoplites beat raiders man for man, raiders beat archers in melee, archers wear raiders down from range, and a tower's archers shoot over walls. Every blow and arrow is drawn: spear thrusts, shield clashes, arrows arcing.
- **Falling and wounds.** A man who falls lies still for a moment and fades. Some of the fallen are only wounded and walk back to the barracks to heal over weeks. A citizen soldier who dies is mourned by his home: a town line, the family's mood, and one fewer person in the house. Companies are refilled the same way they were raised.
- **Breaking.** A side that loses half its men breaks and runs (raiders back to their edge, your companies back to the barracks).
- **What raiders do now (Oliver's choice).** They come in at an edge or from beached ships, march for the nearest stores worth taking and the tax office (where the treasury is kept), carry off goods (up to a share of what's there) and drachmas (up to a tenth of the treasury), set a few buildings alight on the way (the phase 2 fire, which watchmen fight and which can spread, now including houses), then march home. They fight whoever stands in their way. Walkers near them run indoors.
- **Telegraphed as before.** The phase 3 threat, deadline and band on the map stay, now with numbers: "Sparta's band: about 30 men, 4 days away." Bands grow with the ruler's strength and the size of your city: the first is about the phase 3 size (8 to 12); a big city can face 40 or more. A raid always gives at least the phase 3 warning, so there is time to raise and place companies.

**Walls, gates and towers**
- **Walls** are drawn like roads (drag a line), stone, two tiles high, a few drachmas a tile, and only on dry land. Your walkers can't cross them; enemies must go round, or break through.
- **Gates** appear wherever a wall crosses a road: your walkers, traders and soldiers pass; enemies must break the gate down. A broken gate or wall stretch becomes rubble until rebuilt.
- **Towers** (2×2) go on a wall line. Each needs a few staff (archers from the labour pool, paid like workers) and shoots at enemies within about ten tiles, over the wall.
- **Raiders attack walls.** If no gap is near they hammer at the nearest gate or the weakest stretch; a wall stretch has strength and shows cracks as it weakens. That buys time for your companies to arrive.
- **How you built decides how you defend.** A walled town with towers at its gates faces a raid very differently from an open one, and the first raid's route and target are readable in advance: the threat says which edge it comes from.

**From the sea**
- **Landings.** Sea cities (Athens, Troy, and Corinth by sea) can send their raiders by ship: a few dark-sailed ships come into the bay, beach on the nearest open shore, and the raiders land and march inland. The ships wait on the beach and sail off with what was taken.
- **The shipyard** (new, on the shore, like the trading post): builds triremes for drachmas, crewed by rowers from huts and cottages (one man per home, like soldiers, paid monthly). A trireme moors at the shipyard's jetty: long and low, with a bronze ram, painted eyes, a row of oars on each side and a square sail.
- **Guarding the bay.** Triremes at home sail out to meet enemy ships in the bay, and a fight between ships is resolved at sea where you can see it (ramming, oars breaking, a ship listing and sinking). A landing party whose ships are sunk before they beach never lands; one that lands and loses its ships can't carry off what it takes.
- **Guarding the sea lanes.** On the map of Greece you can send triremes to guard a sea lane. **Pirates** now appear on lanes now and then (and enemy fleets in wars), telegraphed on the map ("Pirates seen off Cape Malea"), and can seize your goods on a sea route (a gift, sent goods, a loan's coin, a trader's cargo). A guarded lane is safe; a sea fight there is resolved on the map with a report.

**Loans both ways**
- **Borrowing.** From a city's panel: "Ask for a loan", with an amount (from 500 dr up to what that city can spare). A herald goes, and after the way's travel time the ruler answers: terms (interest and due date) or a refusal with its reason ("Sparta's king does not lend to a city he barely knows"). You accept or decline the terms. The coin comes back with the herald, as coin gifts travel today.
- **Terms, haggled by mood (Oliver's choice).** Each term names what moved it, as prices do: "Corinth will lend 2,000 dr at 12%, repaid in 18 months: cordial (−3%), Corinth is rich (−2%), greedy king (+5%), your city has no debts (−2%)." Richer, friendlier, more generous rulers lend more, cheaper and for longer; greedy ones charge more; shrewd ones want it back sooner; cold ones refuse.
- **Lending.** From a city's panel: "Offer a loan", choosing amount, interest and due date; they accept or decline by need and mood, and say why. Each borrower shows **how likely they are to repay** (likely, fair, doubtful), with its reasons: "steadfast (+)", "at war with Athens (−)", "their harvest failed (−)", "cordial to you (+)".
- **Offers and asks from rulers.** A warm, rich ruler sometimes offers you a loan, more often when your treasury is low or wages are owed. A ruler in trouble (a failed harvest, a war, after a raid) sometimes asks you for one, with their offered interest and date. Like phase 3's requests, these come no more than one at a time per ruler.
- **Relationship consequences.** Lending to a ruler who asked, or on fair terms, lifts their mood ("+15 · lent us 2,000 dr in our need"). Refusing a ruler's request for a loan costs a little, like refusing a request. Being refused when you ask costs you nothing. Repaying on time lifts the lender's mood and makes your next loan cheaper everywhere ("Greece knows you pay your debts").
- **Repayment.** One payment at the due date, principal and interest, through the treasury on its own ledger lines ("Loan from Corinth", "Repaid to Corinth", "Lent to Sparta", "Repaid by Sparta"). You can repay early (in full, interest included). A **debts chip** in the top bar shows the next date due either way, and the Money tab in the side panel lists every loan, with its terms and dates. Loans are limited: one loan each way per city at a time.

**When a debt isn't paid**
- **Your default: escalating pressure (Oliver's choice).** A month before the date a reminder comes, and you can ask for more time (granted or not by mood, at a higher rate). If the day passes unpaid:
  1. A warning, a broken-promise memory with the lender (the slowest to fade), and every ruler hears of it (a smaller memory with each, and every loan's terms worse for a few years).
  2. Two months on, still unpaid: the lender closes their route and seizes your goods on their way: what waits on the quay or in the caravan yard for their trader, and any of your cargo on their lanes, up to what is owed. Each seizure is a town line and a ledger line, and is taken off the debt.
  3. Another two months on: a threat with a deadline for what's left, then an army, larger than a raid band, marches on your city by the phase 3 road or lane. It fights your soldiers as raiders do; if it gets through, it loots up to what is owed and leaves, and the debt is paid off by what it took. If it is beaten, the debt still stands, and another comes after a lull.
  Paying at any step stops the next one.
- **Their default.** When their date comes, a borrower pays (a herald brings it), asks for more time (you grant it or not), or doesn't pay (more likely the doubtful they were). Then, from their panel, you can:
  - **forgive it:** a big lift in their mood and a smaller one with every ruler who hears of it;
  - **demand it:** a deadline, which a ruler who is afraid of you (your army against theirs) is more likely to meet;
  - **send a raiding party:** one or two companies march (or sail, with triremes for a sea city) across the map, take what they can from their fields and stores, and come back: quick, recovering part of the debt as coin and goods, and angering them;
  - **send a full force:** most of your army marches on their city; if it wins it recovers the whole debt with interest and a penalty, and the ruler's anger lasts; if it loses it comes back beaten and short of men.
  Your city stands with fewer defenders while an army is away, and the Army tab and the empty drill ground say so.
- **Expeditions on the map.** An expedition is a little army (or a fleet carrying one) on the map of Greece, its place a pure function of the tick like any traveller. Its fight with the city's garrison is resolved from the two sides' strength (each city has a garrison by its character: Sparta strongest, Troy behind walls, Athens with ships, Corinth middling) and the world's dice. It ends with **a clear report**: what was recovered (coin and goods, carried home and unloaded into the stores), men lost and wounded by company, and how the ruler and Greece took it. Other rulers cool a little at an attack on a debtor, much more at one without cause; marching on a city that owes you nothing is possible, and costly in every mood.

**Saves and other things**
- **Old saves load.** A phase 3 save (version 21) loads with no soldiers, walls or loans; the welcome back says the city can now arm itself and borrow, and where the barracks and the loan button are.
- **Earlier towns.** A town that never builds a barracks, walls or a shipyard and never borrows or lends plays as before, except where Greece now acts on it: rulers may offer and ask for loans, pirates may appear, and raids play by the new rules (loot, burn, then leave, now including the treasury and houses). So `#threat`, `#raid`, `#raiders`, `#aftermath` and `#rivals` change on purpose, and every check-in step that touches them is re-checked. The demos with no routes stay exactly as they were, since nothing in Greece starts before a city's first route.
- **Readable everywhere it touches the city.** Hovering a soldier, a company's banner, a raider, a wall, a gate, a tower, a trireme or an enemy ship says who they are, how strong, and what they're doing. Town lines in their own colour for battles and debts.
- **Sound.** A trumpet when a company is raised or marches, the clash of shields and spears in a fight, arrows' whistle, a gate's thud under rams, oars and a drum for triremes, and coins for loans.
- **Demo links** are bare hash tokens, one or more per slice, listed in each check-in, and each opens from a town prepared at build time.

## What Oliver will experience

**A city that arms itself.** He finds copper in the hills, builds a mine and an armoury, and racks of shields appear in the storehouse yard. He builds a barracks and raises a company of hoplites: sixteen men walk in from the villas on the hill, pick up their armour, and start drilling. A villa says its eldest serves in the 1st company.

**A raid fought in the streets.** Sparta's threat says thirty men from the western road in four days. He pauses, drags the hoplites' banner to the western gate and the archers' banner onto the rise behind it, and plays. The raiders hammer at the gate, arrows fly from the tower, the gate breaks, and the hoplites close. Half the raiders fall and the rest run. Two of his men are mourned in their homes. Another time he is too slow: raiders reach the granary and the tax office, take wheat and 300 dr, set two houses alight on the way out, and the watchmen save the street.

**Walls that decide the fight.** He draws a wall round the old town, with gates on the roads and towers at each gate, and the next raid has to go round to the one weak stretch he left by the river.

**From the sea.** Troy's ships appear in the bay. His two triremes row out from the shipyard, ram one, and the other beaches; its raiders land on the north shore and his companies meet them there. On the map of Greece, he sends a trireme to guard the lane to Athens after pirates take a cargo.

**Credit and its price.** In a lean first year, Corinth's king offers 2,000 dr at 10%, cordial and rich, and he takes it to build his first barracks. Later he lends Sparta 3,000 at 15% when their harvest fails ("likely to repay: fair; at war with Athens"). Sparta doesn't pay. He forgives Athens' smaller debt to win them round, but sends a full force against Sparta: the army marches across the map and comes back with a report of 2,600 dr recovered, wheat in the stores and five men lost. Another year he can't repay Athens on time: a warning, then his oil on the quay seized, then an Athenian army landing on his shore.

**A city worth defending.** By the end, a prepared town has walls, towers, two barracks, triremes, loans each way and a raid on its way, so he can judge whether defending feels like part of the city, and whether debts feel like a choice with teeth.

## Technical design

- **State (`src/sim/state.ts`):**
  - the army: companies (kind, barracks, banner tile, men, order and target), each soldier a walker-like figure with a home (or none for hirelings), health and a fight state;
  - walls, gates and towers as buildings or a wall layer with per-stretch strength;
  - the fleet: triremes with crew, mooring, orders (home or a lane), and enemy ships in the bay;
  - loans (lender, borrower, principal, interest, due tick, terms' reasons, state, escalation step, seizures);
  - expeditions on the map (who, where to, what for, travel phase and start tick, result and report);
  - pirates on lanes;
  - new wares copper and armour;
  - the state version bumped to 22 with a migration from 21 that adds nothing.
- **Modules:**
  - `src/sim/army.ts`: barracks, raising, refilling and dismissing companies, pay, hirelings, banners and marching (reusing the walkers' paths and the off-road walking);
  - `src/sim/battle.ts`: the fight model, ticked only while enemies are on the map (cheap otherwise), deterministic with the city's dice;
  - `src/sim/walls.ts`: drawing walls, gates on roads, towers, wall strength and breaches, and how they change paths;
  - `src/sim/fleet.ts`: the shipyard, triremes, rowers, sea fights in the bay and on the lanes, landings, pirates;
  - `src/sim/loans.ts`: terms, offers and asks, repayments, defaults and escalation;
  - `src/sim/expeditions.ts`: forces on the map, their resolution and reports;
  - raids in `src/sim/raids.ts` change their targets and add the treasury and houses, and their bands fight.
- **Rendering:** soldiers, hirelings and raiders as instanced figures in a few batches (walk, stand in ranks, thrust, shoot, fall), arrows as a small pool, banners, wall and gate and tower meshes that join along a line, cracks and rubble, the barracks, mine and armoury, the shipyard, triremes and enemy ships, all in code. On the map of Greece, armies, fleets and pirates as small figures.
- **UI:** the barracks panel; the Army tab in the side panel; selecting and moving banners (click and drag), with a hint line; the wall tool (drag like roads); the shipyard panel; the city panel's loans section on the map; the Money tab's loans; the debts and army chips; reports on the map.
- **Money:** pay, hirelings, loans, interest, seizures and loot go through the treasury with their own reasons, so the ledger invariant holds.
- **Principles held:** the sim stays pure and deterministic; the renderer and UI only read; every action is a command; nothing on the map the city can't feel.
- **Targets tuned by eye:** company size and pay; armour per set and the armoury's pace; how strong each kind is; tower range and wall strength; band sizes; trireme cost and crew; pirate frequency; loan sizes, rates and dates; the escalation's timing; garrisons and expedition outcomes.

## Testing

Only where it earns its keep:
- determinism: the same seed and commands give the same city, battle and Greece after N ticks, with a raid fought, a loan repaid and an expedition in it;
- independence: a town that never builds a barracks, walls or shipyard and never opens a route plays exactly as before; the earlier demos with no routes unchanged;
- save and load round-trip mid-battle and mid-expedition; a version 21 save loads and plays on;
- the ledger invariant with pay, hirelings, loans, interest, seizures and loot;
- routing: companies reach their banner through gates and round walls; raiders go round walls or break the nearest gate; landing parties reach the shore and their ships;
- battles: hoplites beat an equal number of raiders; archers behind a wall wear down attackers; a side that loses half breaks; nobody fights forever;
- loans: terms follow mood, wealth and traits; repayment, early repayment and default each do what they say; escalation steps come in order and stop when paid;
- budgets: a battle of about 100 figures holds the frame budget; the prepared demos still open under 3 seconds.
